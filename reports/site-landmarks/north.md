# Modelling briefs: northern landmarks (Medford, Melrose, Everett, Malden)

Researched 20 Nov 2026 from text sources only (no images viewed). Conventions:
**[S]** = stated in a cited source; **[E]** = my estimate, not published;
**[U]** = unconfirmed (could not be found in a text source; where I add a recollection of
photographs it is labelled as such and must be checked against the survey shell or a photo).
Feet are converted to metres. Compass directions are those of the sources; check each against
the packaged shell, since several sites sit on rotated street grids.

Best-documented: 3 (Medford City Hall), 2 (Chevalier), 5 (Memorial Hall).
Weakest: 6 (Everett City Hall), 8 (Melrose Highlands), 9 (Pine Banks), and the tower of 4.

## 1. Goddard Chapel, Tufts University, Medford

- **Date, architect, style [S]:** built 1882-83, dedicated 1883; J. Philip Rinn; Lombardic
  (Lombard) Romanesque, "modified Romanesque". Cost $43,777.33. Renovated 1959 (Arland Dirlam), 2002.
- **Walls [S]:** dark blue-grey slate rubble quarried in Somerville; Rinn intended ivy over it.
- **Roof [S]:** red slate (Concise Encyclopedia of Tufts History; "High on the Hill"). Steep gable form is [U], from recollection.
- **Plan [S]:** cruciform "with but one transept carried to completion"; nave plus one transept
  (the "east" transept, to the right of the pulpit, offices since 1959-60). Seating about 490
  originally, about 340 now. No length or width is published. **[E]** nave about 26 x 11 m,
  eave about 7 m, ridge about 14 m; transept about 9 x 8 m.
- **Tower [S]:** "hundred-foot" bell tower (30.5 m), "massive", "plain, blocky", visible for miles;
  bell "Concordia" (c. 1950) and chimes; floodlit since 1952, beacon at the top since 2002.
  **[U]** stage divisions, belfry openings and cap: from recollection, a slender square shaft
  (about 5 m square [E]) with narrow slits, a belfry of paired round-arched openings per face and a pyramidal roof.
- **Cloister [S]:** a triple-arched covered porch linking the transept to the entrance; its roof
  "slopes to a playfully sharp point"; on the side opposite the tower. Since the early 1960s a
  flight of steps leads from it down to the library plaza.
- **Orientation (sources disagree in convention):** Tufts Chaplaincy and Wikipedia put the cloister and
  transept on the **east** side (toward Tisch, formerly Wessell, Library) and one saint window on the
  west wall. "Here and There at Tufts" uses liturgical directions: tower "north", cloister "south",
  entrance "west", chancel "east". Reconciled: nave axis about north-south, chancel at the north (quad)
  end, entrance at the south end, tower on the west flank, cloister and transept on the east flank. **Check against the shell.**
- **Windows [S]:** large round-arched memorial windows at chancel (St Paul) and rear (St John), St Mark on the west wall, two opalescent windows on the east wall.
- **Lettering:** none found [U].
- **Recognised by:** dark slate walls, red roof, tall slim campanile beside Ballou Hall on the hilltop.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goddard_Chapel_(Tufts_University) ;

## 2. Chevalier Theatre (Chevalier Auditorium), 30 Forest Street, Medford

- **Date, architect, style [S]:** the 1939 south wing of the Old Medford High School, by Michael A. Dyer
  (also architect of the 1929 north wing and of City Hall); WPA project; dedicated 7 March 1940 to
  Godfrey de Courcelles Chevalier, naval aviator. National Register (1983) calls its front "Georgian
  Revival"; wings overall Renaissance/Colonial Revival. (The Friends group and Clio call the exterior
  "Greek Revival", interior Art Deco with a double elliptical ceiling.) AI-generated pages naming other architects are wrong.
- **Materials [S]:** red brick, trimmed with cast stone (cornices, pilasters, belt courses, door surrounds), brown sandstone on the 1895 centre block; copper crown mouldings.
- **Massing [S]:** three storeys; L-plan wing south of the 1895 centre block, joined to it by a narrow
  3-bay, 3-storey connector with three double doors in pedimented classical frames; the two wings and
  centre block form an open court facing Forest Street. Assessor: 3 storeys, first floor 14,437 sq ft
  (1,341 m2), gross 56,772 sq ft, brick, flat tar-and-gravel roof. **[E]** footprint about 37 x 36 m,
  parapet about 15 m, stage house a few metres higher.
- **Roof [S]:** flat; the classroom parts of the wings have "pedimented parapets", the auditorium part a cast-stone cornice, copper crown moulding and plain brick parapet.
- **Principal facade [S]:** east, onto Forest Street: a **5-bay pavilion**; ground storey with **three
  entries in cast-stone surrounds**; above them, across the second and third storeys, **monumental
  engaged Ionic columns** framing windows in eared surrounds with moulded caps; **paired Ionic pilasters**
  at the wing's corners; rusticated ground-storey brickwork and belt courses carried over from the old school. Column count is [U] (4 or 6).
- **Tower/cupola:** none.
- **Lettering [U]:** exact text on the building not found in text sources.
- **Interior facts [S]:** about 1,856 to 1,900 seats (floor and balcony), largest auditorium in the Commonwealth when built; Gene Mack Gymnasium in the lower level.
- **Orientation [S]:** school stands at the southwest corner of Forest Street and Hall Avenue; all fronts face east; a semicircular drive, lawn and WWI memorial lie before the centre block; former Vocational School (1929) behind on Bradlee Road.
- **Sources:** National Register nomination, Old Medford High School (1983): https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/991a5ddf-bff6-4af0-9bd6-dbf15169de59/ ;
  https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/MA-01-MD7 ; https://gis.vgsi.com/medfordma/Parcel.aspx?pid=8561 ;
  https://theclio.com/entry/65015 ; https://livingnewdeal.org/sites/chevalier-theatre-medford-ma/ ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Medford_High_School

## 3. Medford City Hall, 85 George P. Hassett Drive, Medford

- **Date, architect, style [S]:** cornerstone 12 Aug 1936, dedicated 11 Sep 1937; Michael A. Dyer
  (M. A. Dyer Co.); contractor John J. Keiliher; PWA/WPA funded, about $450,000. Colonial (Georgian) Revival.
- **Materials [S]:** granite foundation; **first storey ashlar limestone**; upper two storeys
  **water-struck red brick**; rusticated limestone corner pilasters (quoins); limestone trim; cast-iron balconies; membrane roof.
- **Massing [S]:** T-plan, three storeys. Main block **180 ft (54.9 m) wide** facing Salem Street;
  rear wing extends **130 ft (39.6 m) south** (overall 180 x 131 ft). **[E]** main block depth about
  16 m, wing width about 20 m, cornice about 13 m, balustrade top about 14 m, cupola finial about 30 m.
- **Roof [S]:** nearly flat, surrounded by a classical balustrade above a modillion (and dentil) cornice on all sides.
- **Principal (north) facade [S]:** **18 bays** of 12-over-12 sash: limestone lintels at the first
  floor, rubbed-brick flat arches with limestone keystones at the second, without keystones at the
  third. **Centre five bays** are a flat limestone frontispiece: a shallow balustraded stair up to
  **three segmental-arched portals**; above, an **engaged Corinthian colonnade/pilasters** between the
  windows, second-floor windows in limestone architraves with shallow iron balconies; heavy
  entablature; an **empty pediment over the centre three bays**. Set on a slightly raised plaza.
- **Tower [S]:** on the roof just behind the pediment, three levels: (1) square stage with one arched
  louvred opening per face between Doric pilasters; (2) an arch-topped **clock**; (3) **octagonal
  cupola** with small arched louvred/sash openings and pilasters; **small gold saucer dome and gold finial**. Colour of the tower's lower stages is [U] (presumably white-painted).
- **Sides and rear [S]:** east and west ends each have a centre door under a broken segmental
  pediment on consoles with the city seal in a cartouche, and an empty arched niche above; large
  chimney at the southeast corner of the main block. Rear wing: two-storey arched windows (council
  chamber on floors 2-3) with limestone swags; south end pediment with an oculus.
- **Lettering [S]:** exterior plaque beginning "Medford's first Town Hall meetings were held in private homes..." Frieze lettering is [U].
- **Orientation [S]:** front faces north to Salem Street / Hassett Drive across a parking apron; I-93 immediately east; Clipper Ship Drive east, City Hall Mall west.
- **Sources:** MHC Form B MDF.118 (2015, with 1978 form): https://medfordhistoricalcommission.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/mdf.118.pdf ;
  https://livingnewdeal.org/sites/city-hall-medford-ma/ ; https://theclio.com/entry/65021 ;
  https://medfordhistorical.org/mapping-medford/walking-tours/brick-by-brick/

## 4. Melrose City Hall, 562 Main Street, Melrose

- **Date, architect, style [S]:** voted 1873, dedicated 17 June 1874; John Stevens (National Register
  form spells it "John Stevenson"); builders Drown & Stafford; building cost $65,000. Originally
  Second Empire: two storeys plus mansard with third-storey dormers and a clock tower (Howard clock
  and bell, 1874). **Fire** (1937 per NR form and library; one library record says 1936) removed the
  top floor; remodelled 1937-39.
- **Materials [S]:** red brick with brownstone trimmings (Goss, 1902).
- **Present form [S]:** two storeys. **First floor original:** rusticated brick pilasters separating
  the bays, a wide corbelled belt course, segmentally arched windows with elaborate keystones.
  **Second floor rebuilt** in brick, "severely detailed with rectangular window openings and a flat parapet".
- **Roof [S]:** flat behind a parapet.
- **Tower (conflicting):** NR form: the fire "resulted in the removal of the top floor and a rebuilding
  of the second floor and clock tower", and the tower was the town's focal point "until the remodeling
  that took place in 1939". Melrose Messenger (2025): the top floor was "replaced with the familiar
  clock tower we see today", with a mechanical clock. A 2015 library letter says the building is "minus
  the clock tower". Reading: the tall Victorian tower is gone and a lower rebuilt clock tower stands.
  **[U]** its position, height and cap; from recollection it rises at the centre of the Main Street front. Check the shell.
- **Dimensions:** none published; lot 44,934 sq ft. **[E]** block about 22 x 32 m, parapet about 11 m, tower about 18 to 20 m.
- **Facade bay count, entrance treatment, lettering:** [U].
- **Orientation [S]:** northwest corner of Main and Essex Streets; front faces **east** onto Main
  Street; south side on Essex Street; the 1895 fire station (576 Main, red brick, three arched doors,
  campanile hose tower at its southwest corner) is next north, Memorial Hall beyond. A bronze planting urn (1885) stands in front.
- **Recognised by:** Victorian arched ground floor under a plain 1930s upper floor, clock tower.
- **Sources:** National Register nomination, Melrose Town Center Historic District (1982): https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/6d7cf670-aedc-40ef-9885-4a538e93768b ;
  https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/MA-01-MR2 ;
  Goss, History of Melrose (1902), pp. 396-397: https://archive.org/details/historyofmelrose02goss ;
  https://themelrosemessenger.org/articles/2025/local/clock.html ;
  https://bshd8files.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/2018-11/melrose_email.pdf ;
  https://digitalheritage.noblenet.org/s/melrose/item/7172

## 5. Melrose Memorial Hall (Soldiers and Sailors Memorial Building), 590 Main Street

- **Date, architect, style [S]:** cornerstone 30 May 1912, dedicated 14 Dec 1912; George F. Newton
  (Boston); builders McDonald & Joslin; "a geometric adaptation of the neo-classical style". Donor John C. F. Slayton.
- **Materials [S]:** grey **Quincy granite** salvaged from the 1830s Boston (Suffolk County) Court House; ashlar. Roof replaced 1996 with copper flashing; roof form and colour [U].
- **Massing [S]:** "a two-story central block flanked by monumental piers and one-story wings"; the
  granite front "serves as a monument masking the auditorium in the rear". Auditorium seats 800, stage
  30 x 40 ft (9.1 x 12.2 m); G.A.R. room (60 to 100 seats) upstairs in the front section.
  **[E]** front about 30 m wide (centre block about 14 m, wings about 8 m each), centre about 12 m
  high, wings about 6 m; auditorium behind about 22 x 30 m, about 11 m high, plainer walls.
- **Principal (east) facade [S]:** main entry in the central block, reached by **a flight of eleven
  granite steps**; second-storey **transom windows flanked by a colonnade** whose frieze is inscribed.
  Column count and order [U]. Flagpoles stand on top of the building.
- **Lettering [S]:** frieze: **SOLDIERS · MEMORIAL · SAILORS** (confirmed by the NR photo's legible text).
- **Tower/cupola:** none.
- **Orientation [S]:** faces east onto Main Street, north of the Central Fire Station and City Hall; north lobby doors on the north side; city parking lot behind (west).
- **Recognised by:** a pale granite temple-like front with heavy end piers and low wings, the three-word frieze, wide steps.
- **Sources:** https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/6d7cf670-aedc-40ef-9885-4a538e93768b ;
  https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/MA-01-MR2 ; https://www.cityofmelrose.org/493/History ;
  https://macivilwarmonuments.com/2023/05/13/melrose/ ; https://themelrosemessenger.org/articles/2024/09/memhall.html

## 6. Everett City Hall, 484 Broadway, Everett

- **Date, architect, style [S]:** ground broken 1960 (old 1894 hall demolished that year), dedicated
  1962; Harold Michael Turiello (1910-2001) of Revere; Mid-Century Modern / International Style.
- **Walls [S]:** **curtain wall**; spandrel panels were originally **bright blue**, recently replaced
  by **white** panels. Glass, mullion and end-wall materials are [U].
- **Massing [S]:** "four story building" (a lower level plus levels above; two entrances to level 1,
  one to the lower level; two stairs, one elevator). No dimensions published. **[E]** a rectilinear
  slab about 45 x 18 m, about 15 m high, flat roof.
- **Roof:** flat [U but near certain for the type].
- **Entrances [S]:** main entrance on Broadway (two sets of double doors); accessible entrance on the
  Church Street side at the lower level; another from the rear parking lot (49 spaces, "on the
  northwest side"). In 2012-13 a ground-level entrance path, enclosed sitting area and flagpole were built at the Broadway/Church Street corner.
- **Lettering:** [U].
- **Orientation [S/inferred]:** corner of Broadway and Church Street, Everett Square; long front to Broadway, facing roughly southeast, parking behind to the northwest.
- **Recognised by:** a boxy 1960s glass-and-panel office block among the Georgian Revival brick civic buildings of Broadway.

## 7. MBTA Orange Line: Wellington, Malden Center, Oak Grove

Common [S]: Haymarket North Extension; **Brutalist, bare grey board-formed concrete** with angular
forms for stairs and elevators; flat concrete platform canopies over the middle of each platform,
ends open; platforms sized for six 65 ft cars (about 120 m). Architects not named in any source found.
Signage text beyond the station names is [U]; MBTA standard is a white band with black station name and an orange stripe, and the black "T" in a circle.

- **Wellington (Medford), opened 6 Sep 1975 [S]:** at grade; **two island platforms, three tracks**
  (east track unused), single Haverhill Line track further east, no commuter platform. Small fare
  mezzanine **over the north end**, reached by stairs, up escalator and elevator through a narrow
  glazed walkway. Two exits: a short overpass east to a three-lane busway island in the 1,316-space
  surface lot, and the 800 ft (240 m) enclosed "SkyWalk" bridge west over the yard to the Station
  Landing garage, on the heavy truss of the former people mover (1997-2006). Wellington Carhouse and yard lie along the west side. Dominant colour grey.
- **Malden Center, opened 27 Dec 1975 [S]:** **on an embankment/viaduct above Pleasant Street** at
  Commercial Street; one island platform (two Orange Line tracks, west) and one full-height side
  platform for the single commuter track (east), each with a concrete canopy mid-platform, "almost
  entirely concrete". Fare lobby **at street level under the middle of the platforms**, doors on both
  sides to busways (two-lane east, one-lane west with shelter and "Pedal and Park" cage); lobby walls
  **bright orange**. 2003-05 renovation added two elevators and a second exit stair.
- **Oak Grove (Malden), opened 19 Mar 1977 [S]:** at grade (tracks slightly depressed); design adapted
  from North Quincy, more glazed and open than the others; one island platform (canopy covers four
  car lengths) plus a commuter side platform to the east of equal length. Fare lobby is **a bridge
  over the tracks at the south end**; entrances west on Washington Street (stair, elevator tower,
  covered waiting area) and east on Banks Place (glazed footbridge extension, new elevator, covered
  kiss-and-ride and bus loop, 788 spaces). **Orange tile** on stair and escalator enclosures,
  **orange block** elevator shaft. Footbridge east over Spot Pond Brook to Fairlawn Street.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_North_Extension ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_station_(MBTA) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malden_Center_station ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Grove_station ;

## 8. Melrose Highlands station, 497 Franklin Street, Melrose

- **Historic depot: none standing [S].** The first station (c. 1845, originally "Stoneham") stood on
  the west side of the tracks just south of Franklin Street. The Boston & Maine built a larger station
  **just north of Franklin Street in 1903**; it was "demolished in the 1950s or 1960s" (a photograph
  record dated 29 June 1958 shows it still standing at Franklin Street and Marrin Road). Its
  appearance is **[U]**: a colour postcard exists at Melrose Public Library but no text describes it. Do not model a depot.
- **Present station [S]:** MBTA Haverhill Line; **two tracks, two low side platforms**; the outbound
  platform is "a simple slab of concrete" with no shelter; the inbound platform has **a simple shelter
  with a few benches** and a train-arrival screen; **mini-high platforms** (short raised accessible
  platforms with ramps) at the north end of each platform since January 2006; 77-space lot; no
  ticket machines. Shelter material and colour [U] (MBTA standard is a small glazed aluminium or steel-framed shelter).
- **Setting [S]:** immediately beside the **Franklin Street grade crossing** (gates), in the Highlands
  shop district; nearby landmark is the Ella Building on Franklin Street with a tower carrying an LED clock.
- **Position of the platforms relative to the crossing:** [U] (probably north of it, where the 1903 depot stood).

## 9. Pine Banks Park, 1087 Main Street, Malden (Malden/Melrose line)

- **History [S]:** land bought by Elisha S. Converse in the early 1880s (woodland preserve from 1884),
  opened to the public about 1889-90, left to Malden and Melrose jointly in 1904-05; 107.5 acres.
  Wikipedia credits the design to Frederick Law Olmsted.
- **Structures documented in text:**
  - **The Lodge (gatekeeper's house) at the main entrance on Main Street [S]:** MACRIS MAL.233, "Pine
    Banks Park Gatekeeper Lodge"; Goss (1902) calls it "the romantic 'Lodge' at the main entrance"; a
    1910 postcard is titled "Lodge and Entrance". Date, materials and form are **[U]** (MACRIS is
    behind bot protection; I could not read the form). Whether it still stands is [U].
  - **The Log Cabin [S, historic]:** "the rustic 'Log Cabin', situated on 'Island Pond Road', not far
    from the ... 'Lodge'", Mrs Mary D. Converse's private retreat furnished with antiques (Goss, 1902);
    another source says Converse built "several log cabin retreats". Survival, size and look are **[U]**.
  - **Observatory on the summit of Mount Ephraim and a rustic bridge at Island Pond [S, 1902]:** survival [U].
  - "The Bungalow, Pine Banks" (1947 photograph title) [U].
  - **Today [S]:** several large **roofed picnic shelters** (one in the playground covers two long tables), playground by the pond, dog park, track and synthetic fields with lights north of the woods; former zoo removed in the 1990s.
- **Landscape cues [S]:** white pines and cedars, rocky ledges; Mount Ephraim is a bedrock cliff 40 to 50 ft (12 to 15 m) high on the south/east; an esker forms a raised walking path; small pond.
- **Advice:** no text source gives enough to model the lodge or cabin faithfully; a generic open timber picnic pavilion with a gable roof is the only safely documented built form. Get a photograph before modelling the lodge.
