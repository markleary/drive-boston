# Modelling briefs: inner-ring landmarks (Chelsea, Somerville, Brookline)

Compiled 20 Nov 2026 from text sources only (National Register nominations and MHC forms read by OCR, city preservation staff reports, Brookline Historical Society tours, Wikipedia, trade and project pages). No images were consulted.

Conventions used below:
- Plain statements are taken from the cited sources.
- "Estimate" marks a figure no source publishes; treat it as a starting value and prefer the plan and height of the surveyed shell in the package.
- "Unconfirmed" marks a detail I could not find in any text source. Where I add "(recollection)" it is my general memory of photographs, not a citation; check a photograph before building it.
- Compass directions for fronts are derived from the street layout, not from a source, unless stated.

---

## 1. Chelsea City Hall, 500 Broadway (Bellingham Square)

- **Date, architect, style:** Designed 1908 by Peabody and Stearns after the Great Chelsea Fire, completed about 1910 (NR data sheet and Archipedia give 1910). Georgian Revival; the NR nomination says the design recalls Independence Hall.
- **Walls and roof:** Red brick with grey terracotta trim (repairs in 2021 also list precast stone). Hipped roof; roofing material unconfirmed (slate likely).
- **Massing:** 2 1/2-storey main block flanked by one-storey connecting wings. No dimensions published. Estimate: main block about 40 x 18 m, eave about 12 m, tower top 35 to 40 m.
- **Roof forms:** Hipped main roof; hipped wing roofs carrying balustrades.
- **Principal facade:** Slightly projecting central bay dressed in grey terracotta, ornament framing the entry, splayed keystone lintels, heavy modillioned cornice. Bay count, any pediment, steps and door details: unconfirmed.
- **Tower:** Rises from the central bay through the hipped roof; clock faces, urns (missing ones rebuilt 2021), cupola with gilded dome. Stage-by-stage form unconfirmed; (recollection) square brick shaft, white clock stage, octagonal arcaded lantern, gilded dome, in the manner of Independence Hall.
- **Lettering:** Unconfirmed.
- **Orientation:** Sits in the fork of Broadway and Washington Avenue; front faces roughly south-west into Bellingham Square (derived). The rear entrance faces the Chelsea Free Hebrew School, 48 Washington Avenue.
- **In front (Bassett Square):** Soldiers' and Sailors' Monument, Franklin Simmons, dedicated 19 April 1869 in Union Park, moved here 1911: granite base and column (about 33 ft) topped by a standing soldier (NR says bronze). A street clock also stands in the square.
- **Recognised by:** The gilded dome on the brick tower, visible across Chelsea.
- **Sources:** https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/de7a72df-f778-428f-b3e3-41a5861eca2c/ ; https://sah-archipedia.org/node/13565 ; https://www.contextarc.com/chelsea-clock-tower ; https://macivilwarmonuments.com/2020/05/17/chelsea/ ; https://www.discoverchelseama.com/tour-publicart

## 2. Chelsea Square (Winnisimmet Park), Broadway at Winnisimmet Street

Poorly documented in text; the NR nomination PDF (24.7 MB) could not be fetched.

- **Layout:** Two grass areas with Second Street between them; brick sidewalks from a 1977 restoration. Ringed by 19th-century brick commercial blocks that mostly escaped the 1908 fire, including the former Post Office (Chelsea Theatre Works) and an 1898 Police/Court building.
- **Stebbins Fountain:** 1897 (Archipedia: 1898), between the two grass areas. Milford granite (a pinkish-grey granite). Paid for by the will of banker and mayor Isaac Stebbins, which required that both people and animals could drink from it, so expect a street-level horse trough as well as drinking basins. Form and size unconfirmed. Estimate: a granite pier or shaft 4 to 5 m tall over a broad basin.
- **Chelsea Conversation:** Penelope Jencks, dedicated 1978, near the fountain. Three life-size standing figures (a teacher, a track athlete, a girl). Material unconfirmed.
- **General Casimir Pulaski Monument:** Dedicated 22 November 1931, gift of the Polish community; a silver eagle on top. Form of the shaft unconfirmed. A Columbus monument formerly stood on the other side of the square.
- **Bronze Crabs:** David Phillips, 1977, set flush in the brick sidewalk on Second Street.
- **Bandstand:** None found in any source for Chelsea Square. (A gazebo is mentioned at Bellingham Square, near the Mags Harries bronzes.)
- **Civil War monument:** Not here; it stands at Bellingham Square (brief 1).
- **Sources:** https://www.discoverchelseama.com/tour-publicart ; https://www.discoverchelseama.com/post/parks-and-open-space ; https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/MA-01-CL1 ; https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/09000144

## 3. Somerville Theatre (Hobbs Building), 49-55 Davis Square

- **Date, architect, style:** 1914, Funk and Wilcox, for Joseph O. Hobbs. NR form classes it Neo-classical Revival (its text also says "Moderne"; Wikipedia's infobox says Art Deco, which fits the 1932 interior, not the exterior).
- **Walls and roof:** Yellow-grey brick with stone accents; a simple geometric brick pattern on the two street facades; dentil cornice and parapet. Roof unconfirmed (flat behind the parapet is the estimate).
- **Massing:** Three storeys (Archipedia says two). A commercial block with ten original street-level storefronts, offices above, a second-floor hall (now the Crystal Ballroom), and the auditorium (about 900 seats) behind. No dimensions published; take them from the shell. Estimate: parapet about 13 m.
- **Principal facade:** A three-storey ornamental archway at the corner of the front marks the theatre entrance (at the right-hand end, per Wikipedia). Classical detailing on the storefronts. The side facade has five large arched second-floor windows lighting the hall. Upper windows are one-over-one double-hung (wood since 2021).
- **Marquee and lettering:** Original marquee, a large plaque and a roof flagpole are gone. Current marquee dates from 1996 and was made to recall the original. The "Somerville Theatre" sign uses red reverse-channel letters with a changeable-letter board. A "Crystal Ballroom at Somerville Theatre" sign (103 x 30 x 12 in, stainless letters) was proposed in 2021 over the central doorway.
- **Tower:** None.
- **Orientation:** The two main facades face Holland Street / the square and Dover Street; front roughly south to south-west (derived). The MBTA Holland Street headhouse is immediately adjacent.
- **Recognised by:** The tall entrance arch with marquee at the end of a long pale-brick shopfront block.
- **Sources:** https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/98db6445-aa4c-4d6d-a563-5585c2f94e3c ; https://somervillema-live.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/d6archive/sites/default/files/Davis%20Square%2049-55%20-%20%20Staff%20Report%20(POST)_0.pdf ; https://s3.amazonaws.com/somervillema-live/s3fs-public/Davis%20Sq%2049-55%20HPC.ALT%20Staff%20Report%20[POST].pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerville_Theatre

## 4. Davis Square: Red Line headhouses and plaza

- **Date, architect:** Station opened 8 December 1984 (Red Line Northwest Extension); Goody Clancy.
- **Headhouses:** Two, joined underground by a mezzanine running the length of the island platform (east-west under the square). College Avenue headhouse on the east side, with the elevator, an off-street busway beside it and a bike cage east of it. Holland Street headhouse on the west side, next to the Somerville Theatre.
- **Materials and form:** Sources conflict. Wikipedia calls them brutalist concrete; (recollection) red-brown brick walls with a sloping glazed roof over the escalators. Unconfirmed; check a photograph. Estimate: single storey, about 10 x 15 m, 5 to 6 m high.
- **Plaza:** Built on the old Boston and Maine right-of-way between the two headhouses. A barrel-vaulted plexiglass skylight over the mezzanine stands in the plaza. The Community Path continues the right-of-way west toward Alewife.
- **Ten Figures (the Davis Square statues):** James Tyler, 1983. Life-size cast-concrete figures of local people (an elderly couple who kept a restaurant, a mime performing for a teenager and his mother, others). Bronze masks replaced vandalised faces in 1996, when the figures were redistributed in front of the station entrances and in nearby small parks.
- **Seven Hills Park:** Directly behind the Holland Street headhouse. Wind-activated sculptures of scenes from Somerville history sit on tall brick-and-steel towers, one for each of the city's seven hills, with enamel signs. Tower height unconfirmed; estimate 6 to 8 m.
- **Kiosks:** None documented.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_station_(MBTA) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Square ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Square_statues ; https://www.bershad.com/gb/davis-square/DavisSq_moreinfo.html ; https://binj.news/2019/03/07/dilapi-davis-square/ ; https://www.komoot.com/highlight/7211592

## 5. Union Square, Somerville

**Former Engine House, 90-92 Union Square (SCATV)**
- 1903, Walter T. Littlefield. Two storeys of brick bearing wall (Flemish bond in light and dark brick, granite keystones) on a rubble stone basement. Three apparatus bays on the ground floor.
- Four-sided double-hip slate roof with eaves at a flatter pitch.
- Square clock and bell tower at the south-west corner, in the manner of an Italian Renaissance villa: round clock faces, an open top level, now a flat roof. It once carried an octagonal cupola and balcony.
- About 9,050 gross sq ft over two floors, on a 13,200 sq ft parcel at Somerville Avenue and Prospect Street; city parking lot to the north. Estimate: footprint 15 x 28 m, eave 9 m, tower 20 to 22 m.
- Closed 2023; roof repairs 2025.

**Former Main Post Office, 237 Washington Street**
- 1935-36, Maurice P. Meade (Louis A. Simon, supervising). Colonial Revival.
- 2 1/2 storeys, steel frame clad in red brick (Flemish bond) on a smooth granite base raised half a storey; limestone water table, quoins and cornice. Flat-topped hipped slate roof with copper trim; tall brick chimney at the north-east corner.
- South front of five bays with a slightly projecting pedimented centre pavilion; round-arched fanlight over the door; small oriel in the gable. East and west sides eight bays with projecting end bays. Rear seven bays with a flat-roofed loading dock.
- Granite stair of 3 then 8 steps with iron and bronze railings and lantern posts.
- Bronzed letters on the frieze: "United States Post Office".
- North-east corner of Washington Street and Bonner Avenue, on a sloping lawn with a flagpole; faces south.

**Union Square station (Green Line)**
- Opened 21 March 2022. Island platform 225 x 20 ft under a full-length canopy, about 250 ft east of the Prospect Street bridge and 900 ft south of the square. Elevator and stairs up to the bridge; "Passage", a sculptural archway by Matthew Trimble, over the walkway.

**Prospect Hill Monument** (a block north): 1903 stone and iron tower, four storeys, with a lower public deck. (Recollection) crenellated granite; unconfirmed.

- **Sources:** https://s3.amazonaws.com/somervillema-live/s3fs-public/90-92-union-square-exterior-envelope-repair-assessment-2023.pdf ; https://www.thesomervilletimes.com/archives/1900 ; https://somervillema-live.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/d6archive/sites/default/files/Washington%20St,%20237%20-%20Historic%20District%20Staff%20Report.pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Post_Office%E2%80%93Somerville_Main ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Square_station_(Somerville) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospect_Hill_Monument

## 6. S. S. Pierce Building, 1324-1334 Beacon Street (Coolidge Corner)

- **Date, architect, style:** 1898-99 (Wikipedia: 1897), Winslow and Wetherell, for Wallace Pierce. Tudor Revival, drawn from medieval English and German public buildings.
- **Walls and roof:** Stucco with half-timbering over ground-floor shopfronts; steep slate roof. Colours unconfirmed; (recollection) cream stucco, dark brown timbers, grey slate.
- **Massing:** Ground-floor shops, a tall second floor (formerly Whitney Hall, now offices) and attic space in the roof. Asymmetrical. No dimensions published. Estimate: eave 9 m, main ridge 15 m, tower top 22 to 25 m.
- **Roof forms:** Steep pitched roofs with many cross gables and dormers.
- **Principal facades:** Two street fronts (Beacon and Harvard) meeting at the tower. Irregular bays under half-timbered gables. Ground-floor storefronts much altered; do not model them as original.
- **Tower:** Polygonal, at the street corner (the south-east corner of the building), with a clock. Originally taller, with a bellcast roof over an open belvedere. Weakened by the 1944 hurricane; roof and superstructure rebuilt to their present, lower form in 1945 (Wikipedia says a 1948 storm), without the open deck. Present roof shape and number of clock faces unconfirmed; (recollection) octagonal with a pointed roof.
- **Lettering:** Unconfirmed (tenant signs only).
- **Orientation:** North side of Beacon Street at the north-west corner with Harvard Street; long front faces south onto Beacon, the shorter one east onto Harvard (derived). A 1913 block by Page and Frothingham (1336-1354 Beacon) continues west.
- **Nearby:** Two tile-roofed 1901 streetcar shelters in the Beacon Street median at Harvard Street.
- **Recognised by:** The clock tower over half-timbered gables; the emblem of Coolidge Corner.
- **Sources:** https://brooklinehistoricalsociety.org/tours/Coolidge/tour.asp ; https://brooklinehistoricalsociety.org/history/pehlke/coolidge.asp ; https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/85003322_text ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolidge_Corner ; https://sah-archipedia.org/node/13479

## 7. Coolidge Corner Theatre, 290 Harvard Street

- **Date, architect, style:** Built 1906 as Beacon Universalist Church (C. Howard Walker; Romanesque Revival, brick and terracotta). Converted 1933 to an Art Deco cinema, opened 30 December 1933. Conversion architect unconfirmed (sources give Ernest F. Hayward or Louis Hayward).
- **Walls and roof:** Harvard Street front of cream-coloured brick in patterned vertical bands, with herringbone brickwork at the top. Roof unconfirmed (flat is the estimate).
- **Massing:** A narrow street front, with the auditorium (main house about 430 to 700 seats) behind. The original street lobby was sold for shops, so the Harvard Street frontage is storefronts under the marquee. Estimate: front about 15 m wide, parapet 12 to 14 m.
- **Marquee:** Art Deco-style neon sunburst marquee by Mark Favermann, installed about 2002 (one contractor page says 2006), replacing a 1967 marquee that read "The Coolidge Corner Theater". Steel and painted aluminium with polycarbonate, multi-coloured neon. One source gives "42 x 62 foot tall"; which figure is width or height is unclear. Exact lettering and any vertical blade: unconfirmed; an earlier vertical "COOLIDGE" sign on the left was gone by the 1950s.
- **Entrance:** Until 2024, doors and ticket window were in a narrow side passage off Harvard Street leading to the rear car park.
- **2024 addition (Howeler + Yoon):** Three storeys, about 14,000 sq ft, at the rear facing the public car park. Cream brick veneer laid in staggered concave undulations like a stage curtain, with glass walls and corrugated metal panels; glazed third-floor education centre. Now the main entrance.
- **Orientation:** West side of Harvard Street, just north of Beacon; old front faces east, addition faces west (derived).
- **Recognised by:** The neon sunburst marquee over Harvard Street.
- **Sources:** https://brooklinehistoricalsociety.org/tours/Coolidge/tour.asp ; https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/16696-howeler-yoon-aims-for-box-office-gold-with-an-ambitious-theater-expansion-to-a-boston-area-landmark ; https://berkshirefinearts.com/04-29-2008_coolidge-corner-theatre-details.htm ; https://hcmcorp.com/portfolio-items/coolidge-corner-theater/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolidge_Corner_Theatre ; https://www.cinematreasures.org/theaters/215

## 8. Brookline Village (Washington Street at Route 9)

**Fire Station No. 1, 140 Washington Street** (the landmark)
- 1908 (Archipedia: 1907), Freeman, Funk and Wilcox. Called Georgian Revival in the NR nomination, Italianate or Italian Renaissance elsewhere.
- Red brick with white sandstone trim (quoins, lintels, door surrounds) and a copper cornice.
- Three storeys, three bays. Three engine doors on the north elevation. Non-functional balconies at the second-floor windows. Brick parapet around a flat roof. A later extra truck bay replaced a small park beside it.
- Hose tower: square, attached to the east elevation, off-centre. A clock on each face, then a balcony, then round-arched columned openings on all four sides, with heavy brackets under the eaves. Tower roof unconfirmed (low hipped is the estimate).
- Stands just south of Boylston Street (Route 9), front facing north.
- Estimate: body 18 x 22 m, parapet 13 m, tower 28 to 30 m.

**Brookline Savings Bank, 160 Washington Street** (across from the station)
- 1922, F. Joseph Untersee (builder James Driscoll and Sons), on a triangular corner lot at Washington and Boylston. Classical / Beaux Arts.
- Two storeys of Buckeye Gray sandstone. Pilasters carry a stylised entablature and low parapet. Three large round-arched windows in the centre bays of each side elevation; end bays have small aedicule-framed windows under square crossetted ones. A dominant glass dome. Bronze bank-seal medallion over the door.

**Other**
- Brookline Village station (Green Line D), Station Street east of Washington Street: two side platforms with wooden shelters (1959) and raised platforms (2007-09). The 1878 brick station is gone.
- Colonnade Buildings, 207-241 Washington Street (1872-75): three attached three- and four-storey brick blocks, mansard roofs, corbelled cornices, cast-iron Corinthian shopfront columns.
- 2 Brookline Place: eight-storey, 182,500 sq ft medical building beside the station. Facade unconfirmed.
- **Sources:** https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/8eff1e2d-bcce-4e4a-aa1a-800e40020cb7 ; https://brooklinehistoricalsociety.org/tours/Village/tour.asp ; https://buildingsofnewengland.com/tag/brookline-fire-department/ ; https://sah-archipedia.org/node/13492 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookline_Village_station

## 9. Larz Anderson Park and the Anderson carriage house, 15 Newton Street

**Carriage house (Larz Anderson Auto Museum)**
- 1888 (MHC form: 1889), Edmund M. Wheelwright, for William Fletcher Weld II. Modelled on the Chateau de Chaumont-sur-Loire. Said to be the largest carriage house in New England.
- Buff brick with stone trim. Circular towers and a massive roof with many dormers (Archipedia). Roofing and tower-roof shape unconfirmed; (recollection, and as at Chaumont) slate, with conical tower roofs.
- Main hall rises 2 1/2 storeys inside, with brick piers at ground level and a wooden second-floor gallery of staff rooms. Hayloft with dormer hoist openings on the north and west. Basement-level garage added later.
- No dimensions published. Estimate: 55 to 60 m long, 15 to 20 m deep, eave 8 m, ridge 16 m, tower tops 18 to 20 m. Use the shell.

**Temple of Love (tempietto)**
- 1910, attributed to Little and Browne; rebuilt in kind 2021-22 (Klopfer Martin Design Group).
- Open round temple at the far end of the pond: twelve columns each 12 ft tall on a ring of base stones; an entablature band inlaid with rough granite veneer; a dome of 6 in thick concrete shell panels with a balustrade ring at the crown (around an oculus); two flower urns. In front, a basin with a satyr's-mask spout and runnel stones about 5 ft high stepping down to the water, with wing walls and stairs. Terrazzo floor.
- Earth-toned exposed-aggregate concrete. Diameter unconfirmed; estimate 6 to 7 m.
- Two concrete footbridges with balustrades cross the lagoon.

**Other built features**
- Hilltop: site of the mansion "Weld" (demolished 1955); open lawn with a Boston skyline view.
- Jack Kirrane ice rink (1957) on the Italian garden site, with pergola remnants and garden wall fragments.
- Putterham School (c. 1768, moved here 1966); stucco gatehouse at 9 Newton Street (1889); picnic shelter; boundary walls.
- **Sources:** https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/85003245_text ; https://arpa.memberclicks.net/assets/InDepthLookArticles/In%20Depth%20Look-%20Tempietto.pdf ; https://larzanderson.org/history ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larz_Anderson_Auto_Museum ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larz_Anderson_Park ; https://sah-archipedia.org/node/13514 ; https://buildingsofnewengland.com/tag/weld/

## 10. Assembly Row, Somerville

**Assembly station (Orange Line)**
- Opened 2 September 2014 (infill). Ground-level island platform about 410 ft long between the two service tracks, with a third, unused track alongside. White canopy over the full platform length with glazed windscreens.
- Two glass-enclosed footbridge headhouses. North (main): wide stair, up escalator and elevator to fare gates, then a bridge over the inbound track, bike path and Great River Road to a stair, escalator and elevator tower at 499 Foley Street. South (smaller): stair and elevator to a shorter bridge, signed for Revolution Drive.
- Metal-panel mosaic on the facade and benches by Artists for Humanity. Cladding colours unconfirmed.

**Mass General Brigham (Partners HealthCare) campus, 399 Revolution Drive**
- Gensler; built January 2014 to June 2016. 825,000 to 850,000 sq ft; 13 storeys (one source says 14), with ground-floor retail and ten office floors.
- Long side faces south. Seven-storey parking garage with a 1 MW photovoltaic roof. Green roof with a 7,000 sq ft terrace. Nine-acre site with two acres of open space. At the south end of Assembly Row.
- Facade materials and wing layout: unconfirmed. Estimate: 55 to 60 m tall.

**Other**
- Montaje: 20-storey, 236 ft apartment tower (447 units, ADD Inc / Stantec) beside a six-storey block.
- Alloy and the Row Hotel, 360 Foley Street: 13 storeys.
- Sylvester Baxter Riverfront Park (6.1 acres, reopened 2013, Copley Wolff): amphitheatre facing the Mystic, dock, lawn, paths, and a tot lot with a bronze turtle and three bronze frogs.
- LEGOLAND Discovery Center (2014); the giraffe figure outside is recollection, unconfirmed.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_station ; https://subwaynut.com/mbta/orange_line/assembly/index.php ; https://bdcnetwork.com/quick-turnaround-partners-healthcare ; https://hlblighting.com/?p=3101 ; https://www.burohappold.com/projects/mass-general-brigham-at-assembly-row/ ; https://www.bldup.com/posts/callahan-completes-20-story-montaje-luxury-apartment-tower-at-assembly-row-in-somerville ; https://www.copley-wolff.com/all-projects/baxter-riverfront-park ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_Square

## 11. Mary O'Malley Waterfront Park, Chelsea (Admirals Hill)

Thinly documented; no source gives a design date, designer, acreage or dimensions.

- **What it is:** A state (DCR, formerly MDC) park on the Mystic River frontage of the former Chelsea Naval Hospital, taken over after the hospital closed in 1974. Bordered by the Mystic River and the Island End River, with views of the Tobin Bridge and the Boston skyline.
- **Granite pier:** A historic granite wharf (mid-19th century, from the naval hospital) carrying a boat landing and an open shelter. This is the park's one distinctive structure. The walkway has partly collapsed from tidal erosion and the wharf is fenced; DCR was designing a 4 to 5 million dollar repair in 2024. Shelter form unconfirmed; estimate a small open-sided hipped-roof pavilion about 6 x 6 m.
- **Wind sculpture:** By William Wainwright, on the waterfront. Form and size unconfirmed (his work is typically a mast carrying many small reflective pivoting panels).
- **Other features:** A large open lawn on the slope used for concerts, picnics and kite flying; tennis courts; a playground; picnic pavilions; walking and jogging paths; parking. A shoreline path continues past the Admiral's Hill Marina by boardwalk to Island End Park, which has a gazebo.
- **Amphitheatre:** None found in any source; concerts use the open lawn.
- **Orientation:** Waterfront faces roughly south-west across the Mystic toward Charlestown (derived).
- **Recognised by:** The sweep of lawn down to the water with the Tobin Bridge beyond, and the granite pier.
- **Sources:** https://bostix.org/venue/mary-omalley-park/ ; https://recreation.chelseama.gov/business_detail_T14_R1.php ; https://www.discoverchelseama.com/post/parks-and-open-space ; https://chelsearecord.com/2024/08/15/dcr-moving-forward-with-improvement-plan-for-mary-omalley-park-pier/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Hospital_Boston
