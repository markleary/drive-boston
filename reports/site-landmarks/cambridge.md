# Cambridge landmark modelling briefs

Researched 20 Nov 2026 from text sources only (no images viewed). Conventions:

- **Published** = stated in a cited source. **Estimate** = my figure, derived or judged, not published. **Unconfirmed** = I believe it but found no text source; check against a photo before modelling it as fact.
- Feet are as published; metres are my conversions.
- Several good sources could not be read (SAH Archipedia returned 403, the Wayback Machine is blocked, Harvard's property records returned 403), so facade bay counts and lettering are the weakest parts throughout.

## 1. Harvard Square kiosk (1928 subway headhouse, later Out of Town News, now Cambridge KiOSK)

**Dates, designer.** Built Nov 1927 to Jan 1928 over the stairs and escalator of the 1912 headhouse. Concept by MIT engineer Charles B. Breed, architecture by Blackall, Clapp & Whittemore, drawings by Boston Elevated. Dismantled Feb 1981, rebuilt "a short distance from its original site" and roof reinstalled June 1984 (SOM Chicago, Edward Tsoi) as Out of Town News (closed 31 Oct 2019). Renovated 2020-25 (Touloukian Touloukian), reopened 30 May 2025 as the Cambridge KiOSK / visitor centre.

**Plan (published).** Two pavilions in line: north 15 ft x 15 ft 6 in (4.57 x 4.72 m), south 12 ft x 15 ft 6 in (3.66 x 4.72 m); whole enclosure 15.5 x 36 ft (4.7 x 11.0 m), so the former passage between them is about 9 ft (2.7 m). Roof about 30 x 44 ft (9.1 x 13.4 m), 1,350 sq ft: overhang therefore about 2.2 m on the long sides, 1.2 m at the ends (derived).

**Height.** Not published. Estimate: eave 3.0 m, vault crown 4.3 m (Breed's 1925 proposal was 8 ft eaves, 11 ft peak).

**Structure and materials.** Thin corner piers: steel columns clad in alternating bands of dark waterstruck brick (deep red-purple) and limestone blocks (pale buff), like the Harvard Yard gates. Low brick perimeter walls on a polished granite base; everything between wall and roof glazed (originally steel sash with wire glass, 1984 aluminium frames, 2025 "modern windows", thin mullions intended).

**Roof.** Thin standing-seam copper on a riveted iron frame: shallow intersecting ("cross-axial") barrel vaults with flat sections filling the four corners; deep eaves with wooden (cypress beadboard) soffit and shallow rafters; dentilled copper fascia; pendant lamps under the overhang. Landmark guidelines required replacement copper not pre-patinated, so model new brown copper rather than green (unconfirmed that the roof was renewed rather than repaired).

**Lettering.** Semi-circular back-lit signs in the roof arches reading HARVARD SQUARE, red letters on a light ground (pre-1938; restoration recommended). Rooftop signs of the 1940s-60s are gone. Current KiOSK signage unconfirmed.

**Orientation.** Long axis roughly north-south in the plaza at Massachusetts Avenue / Brattle / JFK Street; originally open toward the Coop (west).

**Beside it: Harvard station main headhouse.** SOM Chicago, completed 1984 (lobby opened 2 Mar 1985): low glass-and-steel wedge with a steeply sloped brick rear wall, kept deliberately low for the bank and sunk below the sidewalk in "the Pit" (two curved brick-paved hollows, four steps down). The Pit was demolished March 2025; a level plaza is under construction (city: plaza due end 2026, roadway work to spring 2027); the headhouse remains, with a separate elevator headhouse. Its roof form and colours: unconfirmed.

## 2. Other features in the middle of Harvard Square

Poorly documented in text; treat facade detail here as unconfirmed.

**Harvard Coop, 1400 Massachusetts Avenue (west side of the square, faces east).** Built 1924-25, Perry, Shaw & Hepburn, Georgian (Colonial) Revival. Published: "the traditional brick and limestone"; same width as its predecessor but more than twice as deep, running back to Palmer Street; a 25 ft deep vestibule faced with bronze set "in back of the columns", with a large show window each side; four retail floors; a 1950s awning was removed in the 1990s to restore the "elegant, classical Georgian facade". Unconfirmed (memory): red brick upper storeys over a limestone frontispiece with giant columns framing the recessed entrance, lettering for the Harvard Cooperative Society on the frieze, "THE COOP" signs. Number and order of columns: unconfirmed.

**Johnston Gate (main gate of Harvard Yard, faces west onto Peabody Street / Massachusetts Avenue, between Harvard Hall and Massachusetts Hall).** 1889-90, Charles F. McKim (McKim, Mead & White); first use of variegated waterstruck "Harvard brick" (red with dark, over-burnt headers) with stone trim and intricate black wrought iron. Tall brick piers flank a central carriage gate with smaller pedestrian gates; ironwork overthrow topped by a cross. Two stone tablets on the outer face: one quotes New England's First Fruits (1643: "After God had carried us safe to New England ... dreading to leave an illiterate ministry ..."), the other the 1636-39 General Court orders founding "Harvard Colledge"; exact wording should be taken from a transcription. Seals on the piers and a Latin donor tablet inside. Dimensions unpublished; estimate piers 5.5 m high, central opening 4 m wide, whole composition 14 m wide.

**Cambridge Savings Bank, 1374 Massachusetts Avenue (south side of the plaza, faces north).** 1923. Architect, materials, storeys and lettering: unconfirmed (could not find any text description; do not confuse with the 1897 building on Dunster Street).

Also present: small brick headhouses on both sides of Massachusetts Avenue at Church Street, near Johnston Gate (1983).

## 3. Porter Square: "Gift of the Wind" and the station headhouse

**Gift of the Wind.** Susumu Shingu; commissioned 1983 under Arts on the Line, unveiled 1985; owned by the MBTA. Steel and aluminium.

- Overall published size 14 m x 7.0 m (46 ft x 23 ft): 46 ft is the height, 23 ft the span of the moving top (my reading; the source does not say which is which).
- A tall, slender white pole (estimate: 10 to 11 m to the hub, tapering, about 0.4 m at the base).
- At the top, three large red "wings": flat fan or half-disk shaped metal plates (one source says "3 half-disks", the Globe calls them "lobster claw" shapes with pointed lower ends). They lie on one plane but are twisted to different angles; each tumbles about its own axis while the whole head turns about the pole, clockwise or counter-clockwise with the wind. Estimate each wing 3 to 3.5 m long.
- Colour: originally vivid red. In Nov 2026 it is faded to a dull pink-grey with rust and flaking paint; a restoration (removal, new bearings, blast clean, repaint) is funded in part and not expected before spring or summer 2027. Model it red if you want the intended look.
- Stands in the station plaza beside the headhouse, at about 42.3885 N, 71.1193 W.

**Station headhouse.** Cambridge Seven Associates; built about 1982, station opened 8 Dec 1984; east side of Massachusetts Avenue just south of Somerville Avenue. Published only as a "glass and concrete", "glass-wrapped", glass-covered headhouse notable for its "height, modern materials and irregular angularity", over escalators to a skylit mezzanine and platforms 105 ft down. Unconfirmed (memory): a tall asymmetric shed with a steep single-pitch glazed roof on a white-painted steel frame, rising toward the avenue, over a brick-paved plaza with the other Arts on the Line pieces (granite bollards, split boulders with bronze inserts, the undulating granite "Ondas" wall). Dimensions unpublished; estimate 12 m at the high edge.

## 4. Fresh Pond

**Walter J. Sullivan Water Purification Facility, 250 Fresh Pond Parkway (east shore; entrance front faces the parkway, east; the pond is behind it to the west).** Designed by CDM (Camp Dresser & McKee, project manager Barry Giorgi); on line about 1999-2001 (SAH dates it 2001); $56 million; 54,000 sq ft with purification, maintenance and offices under one roof, on the site of the 1915-22 filtration plant. Deliberately institutional rather than industrial: Romanesque Revival manner recalling H. H. Richardson's waterworks. Exterior of brick with cast stone, granite and slate (so: pitched slate roofs; estimate red-brown brick, buff cast stone trim, grey granite base, grey slate). Arched entrances and arched windows; a two-storey main lobby; an unoccupied tower over the main entrance and a clock tower over a side entrance, both above the 35 ft height limit; a building-length skylight over the process hall. The stone entrance (classical columns and pediment) and two stone medallions with the state and city seals were salvaged from the old plant. Storey count, tower heights, bay rhythm, lettering: unconfirmed. Estimate towers 15 to 18 m.

**Kingsley Park.** A peninsula on the east shore next to the plant, site of the 1796 Fresh Pond Hotel: a wooded rise with two historic stone overlooks toward the water, a grassy "Bowl", pine groves, a lower loop path; renovated 2014-15.

**Neville Place, 650 Concord Avenue (north shore; faces north to Concord Avenue).** The former Cambridge Home for the Aged and Infirm, 1928, Charles R. Greco: Georgian Revival, two and a half storeys, brick over a concrete frame, W-shaped plan (central block with two Y-shaped wings), cast stone pedimented entrance pavilion with a keystone arch flanked by Doric columns.

**Gatehouse.** The city notes a disused gatehouse structure on the reservoir shore; its materials and position: unconfirmed.

**Other.** 155-acre pond; 2.25-mile paved perimeter road; nine-hole municipal golf course with clubhouse on the Huron Avenue side.

## 5. Kendall Square

**Kendall/MIT Gateway headhouse (south, MIT side of Main Street; the inbound entrance).** NADAAA with Perkins&Will, structure by SGH; opened 2023. A wing-like canopy, a rounded parallelogram in plan, nearly 159-160 ft (48.5 m) long, a monocoque shell of fibre-reinforced polymer built by a Maine boatbuilder in two halves, with a keel-like spine cambering its top and snow cleats along the edges; underside of semi-reflective steel panels with light strips. It stands on a field of slender steel columns about 26 ft (7.9 m) tall, thicker near the middle. Beneath are three canted prismatic volumes in fritted glass (stair, escalator, elevator). 2,700 sq ft above grade. Colours not published; estimate white-grey shell, silver soffit, pale glass. Width estimate 9 m.

**Outbound headhouse (north side, in the base of 325 Main Street).** Rebuilt with the 16-storey Google tower (Pickard Chilton); opened 20 April 2026. Glass, street-level windows, two elevators; its roof is the "UP Porch", a public terrace with planting and seating linking Main Street to the Urban Park roof garden. Dimensions unpublished.

**"Galaxy: Earth Sphere" fountain.** 1989, Joe Davis (the Smithsonian inventory lists Piene, Davis and Brigham). In the small plaza where Main Street and Broadway meet, at the east end of the Marriott (42.3624 N, 71.0848 W). Stainless steel, bronze, chrome and granite. A central metal globe with raised landform-like relief; two metal rings around it from which about 20 jets spray inward onto the globe; a circular pool beneath; a circular bench beyond; an outer ring of smaller globes with drilled patterns on open cube frames (lit at night); four "cardinal point" markers set in the paving. Designed to vent low-temperature steam; the steam line has long been broken. No dimensions published: estimate globe 1.5 m diameter, pool 5 m, outer ring 12 m.

**Kendall Square sign; Marriott / MIT Coop plaza.** Could not document a "Kendall Square" sign in any text source: unconfirmed. Kendall Plaza lies between the Boston Marriott Cambridge and 325 Main Street; the MIT Coop was to return to the ground floor and basement of 325 Main (unconfirmed that it has). Marriott tower details: unconfirmed.

## 6. Harvard Stadium (1903)

**Dates, designers.** Built 1903 (first game 14 Nov 1903). Prof. Lewis J. Johnson (engineering), George B. de Gersdorff (design), Charles F. McKim (consultant). National Historic Landmark 1987. Roof colonnade and end pavilions added 1910.

**Material, colour.** Reinforced concrete throughout (steel seat beams), exterior "rough-picked" to remove form marks: a weathered warm grey to buff concrete, smooth mouldings.

**Plan (published).** A U, long axis northeast-southwest, open to the northeast (toward the Charles); N. Harvard Street runs along its southeast side. Exterior 573 x 420 ft (174.7 x 128.0 m). Straight sides 363 ft (110.6 m); closed end a semicircle of 210 ft (64.0 m) outer radius, 115 ft (35.1 m) at the inner parapet; stand 95 ft (29.0 m) deep; field space 481 x 230 ft. Outer wall 1,390 ft (424 m) long.

**Section.** Inner parapet 9 ft above the field; 31 tiers of seats; upper promenade at 50 ft (15.2 m); original outer wall 53 ft (16.2 m); 72 ft (21.9 m) to the top of the 1910 parapet.

**Exterior, bottom to top.** Three steps; two tiers of plain Roman arches on piers, separated by a moulded belt course, with 80 large arched openings in each tier (1903 description), so one bay is about 5.3 m; ground-tier arches are the entrances. Above, the 1910 attic: a parapet wall with a simple square opening over every other arch, under a massive cornice. No engaged columns or pilasters.

**Colonnade.** Seen from inside: a continuous colonnade on the top promenade carrying a flat concrete slab roof, planned as 124 round Doric (Tuscan-like) columns; it ends at each end of the U in a square pavilion/tower.

**Open end.** Closed by steel stands 1929-51; now open, with the Murr Center (1998) standing across it carrying the scoreboard. A long steel-and-glass press box (1981) sits on the northwest stand. Artificial turf and lights since 2006.

**Lettering.** None confirmed on the structure.

## 7. Widener Library (Harvard Yard front)

**Dates, designer.** Ground broken 12 Feb 1913, dedicated 24 June 1915. Horace Trumbauer & Associates; design by Julian F. Abele. Beaux-Arts / Imperial classical.

**Massing (published).** A hollow rectangle about 250 ft long x 200 ft deep x 80 ft high (76 x 61 x 24 m); six levels above ground. Load-bearing masonry, walls three feet thick.

**Materials, colours.** Red "Harvard brick" with Indiana limestone trim, columns, entablature and cornice (pale buff-grey); pale granite steps; flat or low roof behind the cornice (roof form unconfirmed).

**Orientation.** The front faces north across Tercentenary Theatre to Memorial Church; the back faces Massachusetts Avenue.

**Front, as published.** A very broad flight of 27 pale granite steps (estimate: the flight is about 5 m high and nearly as wide as the colonnade, with flanking pedestals) rises to a colonnade of 12 giant Corinthian columns across the front, standing in front of the recessed wall with the entrance doors (wrought iron and glass). The building also has four pavilions with four Corinthian columns each (28 columns in all); their exact placement (ends of the front versus side elevations): unconfirmed. Estimate column height 12 m, colonnade about 45 m wide. The Main Reading Room runs across the front behind the upper windows. Above the main door are carved 15th-century printers' marks (Caxton, Rembolt, Aldus, Fust and Schoeffer).

**Lettering.** The frieze above the columns was designed for "not to exceed fifty letters"; 44 were cut. That count fits THE HARRY ELKINS WIDENER MEMORIAL LIBRARY (36 letters) plus A D MCMXIV (8): this is my inference, exact text and layout unconfirmed. A bronze tablet about six feet square at the outside northwest corner begins "On this spot stood Gore Hall".

**Recognised by.** The steps and the colonnade facing the Yard.

## 8. MIT Great Dome and the Killian Court front of Building 10

**Dates, designer.** William Welles Bosworth; Maclaurin Buildings (3, 4, 10) built 1913-16, dome complete 1916. Beaux-Arts classical; dome adapted from McKim's Low Library at Columbia and, through it, the Pantheon.

**Material, colour.** Indiana limestone (pale grey-buff, weathering grey) over a reinforced concrete structure; the dome is a concrete shell faced in limestone blocks with copper flashings (restored 2009 with 300 new blocks).

**Dome (published).** About 100 ft (30.5 m) in diameter; top 150 ft (45.7 m) above the base of Building 10. A low saucer dome of Pantheon type on a drum, with concentric stepped rings at its base. A 27 ft (8.2 m) oculus at the crown, glazed with 1,042 glass blocks (flat, flush skylight). Interior rotunda 75 ft across and high (Barker Library). Estimate: drum about 9 m high above the main cornice, dome rise about 12 m.

**Portico.** Ten monumental fluted Ionic columns in a single row across the front of Building 10, set on a very slight outward curve, carrying an entablature and a plain attic. Published sources give no dimensions: estimate columns 14 m high on a low flight of steps, portico about 45 m wide, facade height to main cornice about 21 m (four storeys).

**Orientation.** Faces south across Killian Court (grass, trees along the sides) to Memorial Drive and the Charles River; Buildings 3 and 4 (and 1 and 2 beyond) form the U, ending toward the river in pavilions.

**Lettering.** On the friezes/attics of the pavilions around the court, in large Roman capitals with V for U: ARISTOTLE, NEWTON, FRANKLIN, PASTEVR, LAVOISIER, FARADAY, ARCHIMEDES, DA VINCI, DARWIN, COPERNICVS, each with a cluster of related names in smaller letters above (115 names in all). Newton on the east faces Darwin on the west; Lavoisier is on Building 4. Which pavilion carries each of the others: unconfirmed. Inscription on the attic of Building 10 itself: I believe it reads MASSACHVSETTS INSTITVTE OF TECHNOLOGY in one line, but no text source confirmed it: unconfirmed.

**Recognised by.** The dome over the ten-column portico seen across the lawn from the river.
