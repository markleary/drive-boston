# Boston landmark modelling briefs

Researched 20 Nov 2026 from text sources only (no images viewed). Three labels are used throughout:

- **[S]** stated in a cited source.
- **[E]** my estimate or derivation, to be checked against the game's surveyed shells and footprints.
- **[U]** unconfirmed: from general recollection or a single weak source (blog, forum); do not treat as fact.

Metric conversions are mine. Where sources conflict, both figures are given.

## 1a. USS Constitution, Pier 1, Charlestown Navy Yard

**Dates, designer.** Launched 21 Oct 1797, Edmund Hartt's yard, Boston; designed by Joshua Humphreys. [S] Present look dates from the 1927-31 restoration and later work; all guns are replicas. [S]

**Hull dimensions.** [S]
- 305 ft (93 m) overall, flying jib boom to spanker boom.
- 207 ft (63.1 m) billet head to taffrail ("length on deck"). Wikipedia's body text also gives 204 ft.
- 175 ft (53.3 m) at the waterline / between perpendiculars; 145 ft keel.
- Beam 43 ft 6 in (13.26 m). Depth of hold 14 ft 3 in. Draft 21 ft forward, 23 ft aft (22 ft 6 in aft today).
- Four decks: orlop, berth, gun, spar. Hull sides 21 in thick.

**Freeboard [E].** The Navy gives the mainmast as 172 ft above the spar deck and "189 ft above the water", so the spar deck is about 17 ft (5.2 m) above the waterline; bulwark rail about 6 ft higher (about 7 m above water); gun deck port sills about 3 m above water.

**Masts.** Mainmast 172 ft (52.4 m) spar deck to truck. [S] The often-quoted 198 / 220 / 172.5 ft (fore / main / mizzen) are total mast lengths, not heights above deck (they match Humphreys' 1795 totals). Scaling by 172/220: fore about 155 ft (47 m), mizzen about 135 ft (41 m) above the spar deck. [E] Masts are raked aft ("raked masts"). [S] Each mast is three visible sections (lower, top, topgallant/royal) with a fighting top at the first doubling.

**Head gear.** Bowsprit about 64-68 ft long in period tables. [S] Jib boom and flying jib boom lengths not found; the overall minus hull length leaves about 98 ft for head gear plus spanker overhang, so about 80-85 ft (25 m) projects ahead of the billet head. [E] Dolphin striker below the bowsprit cap.

**Yards.** Thirteen: course, topsail, topgallant, royal on each mast (the mizzen's lowest is the crossjack) plus a spritsail yard under the bowsprit. [S] Main yard 94 ft (28.7 m). [S] Others [E], scaled from Humphreys' totals: main 94 / 67 / 44 / 29 ft; fore 84 / 62 / 42 / 28 ft; mizzen 68 / 47 / 33 / 22 ft. Spanker gaff and boom on the mizzen. Sails are normally not bent at the pier. [U]

**Paint.** Black hull with a white gun stripe along the gun deck ports (the "black and white hull"). [S] Copper sheathing below the waterline. [S] Inboard bulwarks dark green. [U, forum] Gun port lid outer faces: sources conflict (white to match the stripe on the restored ship, or black). [U] Lower masts white with black tops and doublings, yards black. [U] Guns black on red-brown carriages. [U]

**Gun ports.** Gun deck: 15 per side plus a bridle port forward, so 16 openings per side. [S] Spar deck: bulwarks pierced for carronades, 11 or 12 per side depending on source (22 or 24 carronades), plus bow chaser. [S]

**Stern.** Flat transom, quarter galleries each side with three windows. [S, historical] A spread eagle (carved 1907, reinstalled Feb 2017) high at the centre. [S] Name "CONSTITUTION" in block letters below the windows. [S] Present window count is not confirmed: the 19th-century rebuild left three cabin windows (one forum says three ports above and three windows below). [U] Decoration picked out in white. [U]

**Bow.** Scroll billet head (no figure) on the cutwater, carved trailboards (new ones carved 2017) highlighted in white, head rails. [S for billet head and trailboards; colour U]

**Deck.** Flush spar deck with bulwarks all round; hatches on the centre line; capstan; double wheel forward of the mizzen; boats on skids over the main hatch and on quarter and stern davits (1812 outfit: one 36 ft launch, four 28 ft cutters, two 28 ft whaleboats, one 25 ft gig). [S for 1812 boats; today's stowage U]

**Standing rigging in outline.** Per mast: shrouds with ratlines to channels on each side, topmast and topgallant shrouds from the tops, backstays aft to the channels, fore-and-aft stays running forward and down (forestay and jib stays to the bowsprit and jib boom, main stays to the foremast foot, mizzen stays to the mainmast). Black (tarred). [E]

**Flags.** National ensign at the stern (gaff or staff), union jack at the bow, commission pennant at the main truck, as on any commissioned ship. [U]

**Berth and orientation.** Alongside Pier 1. [S] She is turned at least yearly so both sides weather evenly, so the bow alternates; "more often than not, the ship was bow-in", bow toward the head (land end) of the pier. [S] No source gives a compass bearing. Pier 1 runs roughly south-east from the yard; Constitution lies on its west side, so bow-in is roughly north-west. [U] Take the axis from the pier edge in the game data.

**Pier 1.** Built about 1902. [S] A mid-pier building with restrooms and food service stands "just above" the ship; Cassin Young and the water shuttle are at the outer end. [S] Dimensions not found.

## 1b. USS Cassin Young (DD-793)

Fletcher-class destroyer, Bethlehem Steel, San Pedro; launched 12 Sep 1943; museum ship since 1978, shown in her 1950s configuration. [S]

- **Size.** 376 ft (114.7 m) overall, beam 39.6-40 ft (12.1 m), draft 13.8 ft. [S]
- **Form.** Flush-deck hull, two smokestacks, tripod foremast (replaced the pole mast in the 1950s refit). [S] Bridge block forward of the first stack with a gun director on top. [E]
- **Guns.** Five single enclosed 5 in/38 mounts (not twin mounts): two forward, superfiring; three aft. [S for five single mounts; positions E, standard Fletcher layout] Two quad 40 mm and one twin 40 mm, one quintuple 21 in torpedo tube bank (one of two removed in 1953), two Hedgehog launchers beside the bridge, one depth charge track at the stern. [S]
- **Colour.** "Grey steel hulled ship"; block numerals "793" on the bow. [S] Standard haze grey with darker decks, white shaded numerals, black boot topping at the waterline. [U]
- **Berth.** Pier 1, toward its outer end, across from Constitution. [S] East side of the pier, bow toward land. [U]

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Cassin_Young ; https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/86000084_text ; https://www.nps.gov/places/uss-cassin-young-cny-wayside.htm ; https://www.nps.gov/bost/learn/historyculture/usscassinyoung.htm

## 2. Museum of Fine Arts, 465 Huntington Avenue

**Dates, designer.** Guy Lowell master plan 1907; Huntington Avenue section opened Nov 1909; Evans Wing (Fenway side) opened Feb 1915; Decorative Arts Wing 1928 (east); White Wing 1970 (Stubbins, west); West Wing 1981 (I. M. Pei); Art of the Americas Wing 2010 (Foster + Partners). [S]

**Material.** Grey granite; one source says Deer Isle (Maine) granite. [S] Colour: pale cool grey. [E]

**Huntington Avenue facade.** "500-foot (150 m) facade of granite". [S] Classical Revival / Beaux-Arts: a raised central Ionic temple portico "set back from the street", repeated as a motif on projecting side wings, two symmetrical wings either side. [S] The central portico has "four huge Ionic columns" under a pediment. [S, one source] Reading bay by bay, west to east [E]: projecting end pavilion with columns - long two-storey wing wall with tall windows - recessed central block with the tetrastyle portico on a flight of steps - wing - end pavilion. The projecting wings enclose a lawn forecourt open to the avenue. [E] Low attic and flat or low-pitched roofs behind a parapet; a low dome over the rotunda behind the portico is barely visible from the street. [E] Height to cornice about 15-18 m. [E]

**Lettering.** "MUSEUM OF FINE ARTS" on the portico frieze. [U]

**Orientation.** Faces Huntington Avenue, roughly south-east; the Fenway facade faces north-west across the Fens. [E]

**Appeal to the Great Spirit.** Cyrus Dallin, bronze, made 1908, installed 23 Jan 1912 on the front lawn outside the main entrance, on the central axis, facing the avenue. [S; axis and facing E] Bronze 290 x 250 x 300 cm (which is height is not stated; about 2.9 m high is likely). [S] Mounted rider, arms spread low and wide, head tipped back to the sky; horse standing square with head lowered. [S; horse pose U] Granite pedestal about 2 m high. [E]

**Fenway (Evans Wing) facade.** "A monumental Ionic colonnade extending along the entire length and height" of the wing, 1911-15. [S] Column count not found; about 22 giant-order columns over a rusticated basement, wing about 150 m long. [U/E] Fenway entrance at the centre, reopened 2008. [S]

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Fine_Arts,_Boston ; https://www.mfa.org/about/architectural-history ; https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/MA-01-FL12 ; https://mass.historicbuildingsct.com/?p=271 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeal_to_the_Great_Spirit

## 3. Jamaica Pond Boathouse and Bandstand, 507 Jamaicaway

**Dates, designer.** William Downes Austin (Stickney & Austin), built 1910-12 (city: 1912; TCLF and SAH: 1913). "Tudor-Bungalow style." Partial renovations 1970s and 1990s; plaza and paths rebuilt 2019. [S]

**Setting.** East shore of the pond, at the foot of Pond Street on the Jamaicaway, on the shoreline path. [S] The two buildings stand side by side along the shore with a paved plaza and historic stormwater planters between and around them; the plaza is on the axis of Pond Street, with the pond straight ahead. [S for plaza and planters; axis E] Which building is north and which south was not confirmed; identify them from the footprints (the boathouse is the larger and has the docks). [U]

**Boathouse.** 1.5 storeys. [S] "Half-timbered." [S] Painted masonry (brick is mentioned at the south-west corner) with wood trim and railings. [S] Stucco or painted brick lower walls, cream to buff, with dark brown half-timbering in the gables. [E] Steep gabled roof of terracotta tile (red-orange), copper gutters. [S] Ground floor: boat rental, "fireplace room" (so a chimney), public restrooms; an upstairs room in the roof. [S] Docks on the pond side. [E]

**Bandstand.** "1 story + open air pavilion": an enclosed lower level (ranger station, nature centre) at shore level with an open, roofed pavilion above, used for concerts and dancing and prized for its view of the pond. [S] Same tile roof, carried on timber posts with railings. [E]

**Pergola.** No source consulted mentions a pergola connecting the two; treat as unconfirmed and check the footprints or a photograph. [U]

**Dimensions.** Not published in the sources found. Each building roughly 12 x 20 m, ridge about 9 m (boathouse) and 8 m (bandstand). [E]

**Recognised by.** The pair of red tile roofs on the water's edge, rowboats and small sailboats at the dock.

Sources: https://boston.gov/departments/parks-and-recreation/project/jamaica-pond-boathouse-study ; https://content.boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2026/06/JPondBoathouse_Meeting1-Presentation_260415.pdf ; https://www.emeraldnecklace.org/poi/jamaica-pond-boathouse-bandstand ; https://www.tclf.org/landscapes/jamaica-pond ; https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/MA-01-JP6

## 4. Arnold Arboretum: Hunnewell Building and Arborway Gate

**Hunnewell Building.** Alexander Wadsworth Longfellow Jr. (Longfellow, Alden & Harlow), built 1892 with funds from H. H. Hunnewell (NPS gives 1903). [S] "Grand brick structure", "dark brick with sandstone trim". [S] Stands just inside the Arborway entrance. [S]

- **Massing.** Original front block with a hip roof over a finished attic and a full basement; a three-storey link; a four-storey flat-roofed rear section added within a few decades, which nearly doubled the building. [S] A stair and elevator tower was added in 1993. [S]
- **Roof.** Hip roof in "North Country Black" slate with snow guards, copper gutters, restored masonry cornice; rear roof flat, white membrane. [S]
- **Floors.** First: visitor centre and lecture hall; second: herbarium; third: library and archives. [S] So the front block reads as two main storeys on a raised basement plus the attic. [E]
- **Facade.** Symmetrical, central front entrance under the slate roof, windows with jack arches and stone lintels. [S for entrance, jack arch, lintels; symmetry E] Roughly five bays, about 22 x 13 m, eave about 10 m, ridge about 14 m. [E] Long ivy-covered in old photographs; ivy since removed. [S]
- **Colour.** Dark red-brown brick, brown sandstone trim, black slate, green copper. [S/E]

**Arborway Gate.** One of the original gates; contract early autumn 1898, in place by June 1899; Longfellow drew the gate plans. [S] Boundary walls of roughly dressed Roxbury puddingstone curve inward to pedestrian entrances; square piers of puddingstone with contrasting fine-grained bluestone, taller piers flanking the carriage drive. [S] Broad swinging wrought-iron gates with a small flourish of scrollwork and a pineapple-like finial. [S] A bronze National Historic Landmark plaque (May 1966) on one pier. [S] Pier height about 3 m, carriage opening about 7 m. [E] The gate opens on the Arborway; the building is immediately to the right (west) of the drive inside it. [U]

Sources: https://arboretum.harvard.edu/stories/hunnewell-building-renovations-preserve-history-sustain-functionality ; https://arboretum.harvard.edu/timeline/hunnewell-building-constructed ; https://arboretum.harvard.edu/stories/welcoming-the-public-the-arboretums-entrances ; https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/MA-01-JP4 ; https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/photo-safari-arnold-arboretum.htm

## 5. Franklin Park Zoo

Zoo laid out by Arthur Shurcliff (Shurtleff), opened 4 Oct 1912. [S]

**Zebra Entrance (main, east).** Off Blue Hill Avenue near Peabody Circle and Columbia Road, with the main parking lot between Franklin Park Road and Blue Hill Avenue. [S] Built 1918, at the end of The Greeting promenade; "marked by eight classical granite columns taken from the Custom House Tower before their removal in 2007"; smaller gates added 1934. [S] The present gate is a modern ticket plaza with booths and fencing. [U] No description of zebra sculpture was found. [U]

**Giraffe Entrance (north-west, Playstead side).** On Pierpont Road, opening to the interior of Franklin Park. [S] "Framed by two marble statues designed by Daniel Chester French." [S] A giraffe statue stands at the entrance. [S, zoo post]

**French statue groups.** Two groups carved for the old Boston Post Office (erected there 1885), removed 1929, unveiled at the park 17 Sep 1930 on granite pedestals designed by Shurcliff. [S] White Vermont marble, each of three interlocking figures. "Labor" is 15 ft (4.6 m) high; "Science" is the same scale. [S for Labor; Science E] Pedestals about 3 m. [E]
- *Science Controlling the Forces of Electricity and Steam*: seated woman, a chained giant crouched at her feet beside a locomotive wheel, a youth with a thunderbolt standing at her right.
- *Labor Sustaining Art and the Family*: seated smith in a leather apron, arm on an anvil that shelters a mother and baby, a figure of Art at his right. [S]
They flank the entrance and face down the zoo's former allée (toward the zoo, roughly south-east). [S; compass E]

**Tropical Forest pavilion.** DiMella Shaffer Associates, engineers Weidlinger Associates; opened 9 Sep 1989. [S] Arch-supported membrane roof with a cable net: PTFE (Teflon) coated glass-fibre fabric, white and translucent. [S; colour E] Height 23 m; membrane area 3,800 m2; building area 4,500 m2; floor area 6,500 m2; described as a "3-acre building". [S] Plan roughly round to oval, about 75 m across, a low concrete perimeter wall with the fabric rising in peaked lobes between steel arches. [E]

Sources: https://www.tclf.org/landscapes/franklin-park-zoo ; https://www.jphs.org/people/2005/3/14/the-statues-of-dc-french-from-the-old-boston-post-office-at.html ; https://structurae.net/structures/tropical-forest-pavilion ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Park_Zoo ; https://www.zoonewengland.org/franklin-park-zoo/hours-info-faq/

## 6. Chestnut Hill Reservoir

Naming note: the Landmarks study lists **two gatehouses on the embankment of the present basin**, Effluent Gate House No. 1 and Effluent Gate House No. 2. The **Intermediate Gate House** is a third, separate building on the old dam between the two basins, now on the edge of Boston College's playing field, not on the present shore. [S]

**Effluent Gate House No. 1 (1869-70).** On the edge of the basin near Cleveland Circle (east end). [S] Two-level square structure with a hip roof (slate) and a central ventilator at the apex (cap lost). [S] Dressed granite ashlar in random rangework with darker rock-faced quoins and window surrounds; three bays across; round-headed windows, bricked in. [S] Granite foundation above the water line and a "monumental granite double stairway facing Cleveland Circle". [S] About 9 x 9 m, eave 6 m above the path. [E]

**Effluent Gate House No. 2 (about 1898-1901, Wheelwright & Haven).** On the embankment directly across Beacon Street from the High Service station (south shore). [S] One storey, three window bays across and one deep; Renaissance Revival; rusticated banding of dressed granite ashlar, iron window grilles, low-pitched copper-clad hip roof (green) with bronze cresting; entrance gate and steps. [S] About 12 x 6 m. [E]

**Intermediate Gate House (1866-70).** "Simple looking granite block"; hammered granite, rectangular, about 30 x 25 ft. [S]

**High Service Pumping Station (Waterworks Museum), 2450 Beacon Street.** Arthur H. Vinal, City Architect, 1887-88; west addition by Wheelwright & Haven 1897-99. Richardsonian Romanesque. South side of Beacon Street facing the reservoir (faces north). [S]
- **Massing.** Horizontal, 2.5 storeys: "three main gable roofs rise in stages from east to west", a 112 ft (34 m) hipped-roof tower with an open observatory in front, east of centre, and a 150 ft (46 m) red brick smokestack behind. [S]
- **Parts, east to west.** Coal pocket 62 x 65 ft 4 in (lowest, far left); boiler room 79 ft 10 in x 56 ft 2 in; engine room 84 ft 10 in x 64 ft 8 in with the central gabled entrance pavilion and a broad round ("Syrian") entrance arch offset right; the 1897 gabled wing at the right, dated "1897" on its west cross gable. [S] Total frontage about 85-90 m. [E]
- **Materials.** Rock-faced pink Milford granite in random ashlar; bold reddish-brown Longmeadow freestone bands and surrounds ("primarily pink and brown"); blue-grey slate roofs with oxidised copper flashing and cornices; rear elevation red brick. [S]
- **Windows.** Rectangular and arched, in groups of two and three. [S]
- **Details.** Carved faces of Vinal and his wife on the tower. [S]
- **Lettering.** Bronze sign over the door: "Metropolitan Water Works". [S]

Sources: https://www.cityofboston.gov/images_documents/Chestnut%20Hill%20Reservoir%20Pump%20Stations%20Study_tcm3-19719.pdf ; https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2018/01/11/chestnut-hill-rmp.pdf ; https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/MA-01-AB4 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Waterworks_Museum ; https://www.theclio.com/entry/69078

## 7. MBTA stations

Text sources describe these thinly; most appearance details below are [E] or [U].

**Mattapan (trolley and bus).** At Mattapan Square, River Street and Blue Hill Avenue, beside the Neponset River and trail. [S] Southern terminus of the Mattapan Line: a balloon loop with one side platform for alighting and one island platform for boarding (the loop track splits in two there); a streetcar yard and enclosed maintenance shed (about 2000); bus loops (north and south) for eight routes. [S] Rebuilt 2006-07: the 1929 shelter was replaced by "modern platforms with canopies" and a new building for MBTA police and bus operations with a community room; reopened 22 Dec 2007. [S] Canopies are flat, steel-framed, "gas station" like. [S, blog] Low mini-high platforms. [S] Colours: grey or dark green steel, red-brown brick building. [U] The cars are orange and cream PCC streetcars. [S for orange] "The Loop at Mattapan Station" (2023, 135 apartments over retail) stands on the former parking lot. [S]

**Maverick (East Boston).** Blue Line; island platform below, surfacing "in the middle of Maverick Square". [S] The 2005-09 rebuild replaced the Maverick Square headhouse, added a second headhouse on Lewis Mall (opened 22 Sep 2007) and a dedicated busway in the square. [S] The square headhouse is "very sleek and modern, mostly glass", with a large T roundel set in the paving in front; the busway has benches but no proper shelters. [S, blog] A design proposal had clipper ships etched on alternate glass panels. [S, proposal] Form: a long single-storey glass and steel pavilion, about 10 x 30 m and 5 m high, with a flat overhanging roof. [U/E]

**Nubian (formerly Dudley), Roxbury.** At Washington and Dudley Streets. [S] The 1901 Boston Elevated terminal by A. W. Longfellow Jr., Beaux-Arts / French Renaissance, "clad in copper with an internal arched structure", was lowered 12 ft to the ground and rolled 180 ft south (784,000 lb) and reopened in late 1993 as the bus station. [S] Bus platforms A, B, C run north-south (under the relocated building); D, E, F run east-west under new shelters "in a similar style". [S] Appearance: long open sheds with curved, arched steel ribs and hipped copper roofs gone green, glazed lanterns, on slender columns over brick-paved islands. [E]

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattapan_station ; https://ranyasramblings.blogspot.com/2022/09/station-review-7-mattapan-mbta.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maverick_station ; https://milesintransit.com/?p=964 ; https://www.behance.net/gallery/2776135/Maverick-Square ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubian_station ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Station_Historic_District

## 8. Brigham Circle and Downtown Crossing

**Brigham Circle.** Junction of Huntington Avenue, Tremont Street and Francis Street in Mission Hill; never a true rotary; the Green Line E branch runs in the avenue, with Brigham Circle station just east. [S]
- **Peter Bent Brigham Hospital administration building** (Codman & Despradelle, 1911-13; 15 Francis Street / 721 Huntington Avenue): the circle's "definitive landmark", approached across Stoneman Centennial Park. [S] Classical Revival; the "main south-facing entry" has "six monumental columns supporting a large triangular" pediment, doors centred. [S] SAH calls it "Doric-porticoed". [S] Walls: one source says red brick; colour not otherwise confirmed (it may read pale). [U] Two to three storeys, about 40 m wide, low roof with a shallow dome over the rotunda. [E] The modern hospital towers rise behind.
- **Also in view.** A street-level park with metal benches (farmers' market). [S] One Brigham Circle, a 2000s brick retail and office block on the Tremont Street side. [U] The twin puddingstone spires of the Mission Church (Basilica of Our Lady of Perpetual Help) up Tremont Street. [U]

**Downtown Crossing.** Washington, Winter and Summer Streets; pedestrian mall since 1979. [S]
- **Burnham Building (Filene's), 426 Washington Street.** Daniel H. Burnham & Co., 1911-12, his only Boston work; Beaux-Arts. [S] Eight storeys above ground; steel frame clad in glazed terra cotta and brick; facades on Washington and Summer Streets (block also bounded by Hawley and Franklin). [S] Composition [E]: two-storey glazed shopfront base, five storeys of wide three-part windows between slender piers, an attic storey and a deep projecting cornice. Terra cotta colour grey to grey-green. [U] Tenants: Primark (floors 1-4), Roche Bros. (basement, Summer Street entry). [S] Original lettering not confirmed. [U]
- **Millennium Tower, 1 Franklin Street.** Handel Architects (Blake Middleton), 60 storeys, 684 ft (208 m), opened 2016; glass curtain wall; rises directly behind the Burnham Building on the rest of the Filene's block. [S] Faceted, tapering blue-grey glass shaft. [U] A plaza with a station entrance and stepped seating fronts it on Washington Street. [S, blog]
- **Clock / kiosk.** "An ornate street clock alongside Washington Street" near that plaza. [S, single blog] A four-faced cast-iron post clock about 5 m tall is likely. [E] No source confirmed an information kiosk; a flower stand stands at Washington and Winter. [S, blog]
- **Macy's (former Jordan Marsh)** on the opposite corner of Summer Street, with a station entrance in its facade. [S]
