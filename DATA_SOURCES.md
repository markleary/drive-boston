# Boston source data: connected central neighborhoods

This prototype uses real Boston building meshes and mapped street geometry. Its façades, road widths, lane paint, street furniture, and landmark embellishments are interpretive game art, not a survey-accurate reconstruction.

## Included geography

The curated coverage is approximately **10.02 km²**. It combines the City of Boston's official GIS polygons for **Back Bay, Mission Hill, Fenway, Longwood, Beacon Hill, Downtown, Chinatown, Bay Village, and Leather District**, retaining the original Mission Hill study fringe. An 18-meter margin around the outer edge keeps boundary streets and junctions whole. There are no internal district loading seams. Fenway is the official GIS label for the neighborhood containing Kenmore Square; the map includes a separate Kenmore Square destination. Longwood, Bay Village, and Leather District complete adjoining sections rather than leaving gaps between the requested neighborhoods.

The naming authority is the [City of Boston basemap Neighborhoods layer 16](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/16), retrieved October 27, 2026. Its polygons place Boston Common and the Public Garden within Beacon Hill. The City's [Boston Common](https://www.boston.gov/parks/boston-common) and [Public Garden](https://www.boston.gov/parks/public-garden) pages instead use the neighborhood label Downtown. The game consistently uses the GIS polygons for neighborhood lookup and preserves both parks as individually named destinations; neither park is omitted because of naming differences.

The larger study envelope is west −71.116, south 42.326, east −71.047, north 42.3645. `city.coverage` records the outer playable outline, while `city.neighborhoods` records the official polygons, including holes. The larger terrain rectangle is not a claim of complete building coverage. The package contains **3,763 real building meshes, 2,259 road paths, 3,248 curb paths, 265 signal locations, 231 park polygon parts, 21 merged water polygons, and 600 park-path segments**. A park can contain multiple polygon parts, so 231 is not a count of separate parks. All **1,973** building IDs from the previous Back Bay–Mission Hill build remain included. All 14 destinations have a connected road route (including the Bowker upper roadway) from the original Back Bay spawn.

- **Buildings:** [Boston Planning 3D downloads](https://www.bostonplans.org/3d-data-maps/3d-smart-model/3d-data-download), June 2026 release. The download page dedicates these downloads under the Open Data Commons **PDDL**. Included models come from 12 tiles: BOS_E_6, BOS_F_4, BOS_F_5, BOS_F_6, BOS_G_4, BOS_G_5, BOS_G_6, BOS_H_3, BOS_H_4, BOS_H_5, BOS_I_4, BOS_I_5. The downloader finds 18 tiles intersecting the larger envelope, then the compiler omits models outside curated coverage. The exact source URL pattern is `https://maps.bostonplans.org/3d/Bos3d_BldgModels_20260624_OBJ/BOS_G_4_BldgModels_OBJ.zip` (substitute the tile ID).
- **Street centerlines:** [Boston Infrastructure/OpenData layer 8](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/8). Original geometry, names, and one-way direction flags are retained. [Boston's street-segments metadata](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=8f31b239f40847e29543322093c717fc) declares PDDL.
- **Curbs:** [Boston Infrastructure/OpenData layer 6](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/6).
- **Signal locations:** [Boston Infrastructure/OpenData layer 12](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/12).
- **Terrain:** [USGS 3DEP Bare Earth DEM ImageServer](https://elevation.nationalmap.gov/arcgis/rest/services/3DEPElevation/ImageServer), exported October 27, 2026. The source supplies numeric elevation rather than a hillshade picture. [USGS 3DEP products](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program) are available without use restrictions. The raw float32 TIFF, full export request, grid parameters, vertical reference, and source hash are recorded by the preprocessing scripts; provenance is bundled with the game.
- **Neighborhood polygons:** [Boston basemap layer 16](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/16), using the `name` field and complete official polygon rings.
- **Parks:** [Boston basemap layer 14](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/14), using `SITE_NAME` and `TypeLong`. Boston Common, Public Garden, Back Bay Fens, Commonwealth Avenue Mall, and smaller public spaces are included. Park geometry is clipped to coverage; existing Copley Square art remains separately authored.
- **Water:** [Boston basemap Hydro polygons layer 12](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/12), supplemented by OpenStreetMap pond details. Overlapping outlines are geometrically united before packaging to avoid drawing two competing water surfaces. River and harbor geometry is clipped to the terrain envelope, so water remains visible immediately beyond the developed area.
- **Park paths and additional water detail:** [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), © OpenStreetMap contributors, **ODbL 1.0**. Small map extracts were retrieved October 27, 2026 from the official OSM API around the Common/Garden, Fens, and Commonwealth Avenue Mall. The raw source, actual request boxes, and method are cached. Footway/path/pedestrian/step centerlines are clipped to official park polygons; tagged bridge segments retain that attribute. Path widths are source tags where present and otherwise documented game assumptions. No OSM map tiles or photographs are bundled. The derived open geographic data and source attribution remain available in the static package and preprocessing scripts.

- **Road inventory inspected, not used as current authoritative lane data:** [layer 14](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/14). Some fields are blank, zero, or carry old dates. The prototype therefore uses documented width assumptions instead of presenting them as current lane measurements.

Street GIS requests use the selected envelope, `inSR=4326`, `outSR=4326`, `outFields=*`, and `spatialRel=esriSpatialRelIntersects`. Object IDs are fetched first and features downloaded in batches to avoid silent record-limit truncation. Raw data is cached for preprocessing rather than requested by each player. Attribution is retained in the application's credits and in `public/data/city.json`.

## Deliberate limitations

- The June 2026 publication date is **not** a fresh survey of every building. Most selected building shapes originate in 2011 observations. Per-model source survey dates, methods, status, and tile IDs are retained in `public/data/provenance.json`.
- A mesh can span several attached addresses or represent one building part. It is not necessarily one parcel or one address.
- `Current`, `Complete`, and `Approved Demo` buildings are included. An approved demolition is still standing according to the catalog. Historical, merely approved, and under-construction models are omitted. Low ancillary walls are omitted. This avoids drawing historical/proposed structures over existing buildings.
- Downloaded OBJ exports contain no photographic façade textures. Runtime façades are original procedural materials. No Google, Nearmap, or Mapillary photographs are bundled.
- Storefront glazing, signs, and awnings are original interpretive art. Their labels are fictional, and their street-facing placement is checked against the real building mesh; they do not reproduce actual businesses.
- Roof-mounted air-conditioning units and fans are original interpretive props. Their full clearance area is checked against flat source roof triangles; they do not claim to reproduce actual rooftop installations.
- Projecting window trim, cornices, stoops, and railings are original game geometry attached to compatible source surfaces. The window grid follows the procedural material coordinates; these details are not individual building surveys.
- Copley Square landscaping interprets the [current Boston Parks illustrative site plan](https://www.boston.gov/departments/parks-and-recreation/project/copley-square-park-improvements), reviewed October 26, 2026. Raised grove, fountain-side groves, plaza, small Trinity-side lawn, and seating are approximate original meshes aligned to the surrounding road grid. No plan images are bundled, and this is not a measured as-built landscape model.
- Player/traffic models, pedestrian figures, foliage, and props are original procedural art. Parking placement and pedestrian routes are checked for road/building clearance; they do not claim current legal parking rules or measured footfall.
- Sound effects were generated with ElevenLabs, not captured in Boston. Prompts, durations, model ID, and asset hashes are retained in `public/audio/provenance.json`; the API key is used only by the local generation script.
- Mission Hill's grades come from the **actual bare-earth DEM**, packaged at 6-meter spacing. Bilinear export resampling and a gentle 3×3 `[1,2,1]` separable smoothing pass suppress small retaining-wall/kerb aliasing; these are not centimeter-accurate road surfaces or constructed ramps. The local terrain reaches approximately 63.55 meters above the Back Bay starting datum. The Parker Hill Avenue destination is approximately 58.82 meters above it.
- Buildings retain source horizontal placement and relative massing. An audit found that the catalog's historical `Gnd_El_Ft` field contains metre-scale ground estimates, while OBJ coordinates are feet. New-area primary models use metre-correct ground normalization, with their actual source floor seated at the highest DEM sample around the footprint minus 0.1 meter. Runtime foundation skirts close the downhill gap. This is a practical terrain seating approximation, not an as-built foundation survey; large sloping sites may have visible retaining bases.
- **Supported upper parts remain elevated:** 75 source components share a support model's translation, rather than being individually dropped to the street. Their `partOf` relationship requires at least 90% footprint support and compatible original vertical extents. The validator independently checks both. Low ancillary meshes are omitted. Three additional central-city source files contain datum/helper vertices or collapsed underground triangles; normalization uses the lowest surviving visible face to prevent these helpers from leaving the building suspended above its base.
- The original 163 Back Bay models retain their legacy vertical normalization (the expanded source set includes one additional source component inside that same compatibility rectangle) so the already authored landmark wall planes and silhouettes remain unchanged. This compatibility policy, the catalogue value, selected normalization ground in meters, original source min/max elevations, and any ground-contact offset are recorded per model in `public/data/provenance.json`.
- **Selected Bowker/Charlesgate structures are now independent upper roadways:** 30 retained City GIS centerline segments restore the Bowker carriageways/ramps and connecting Boylston bridge approaches, with two additional existing Commonwealth Avenue paths replaced by lower bridge decks across the Muddy River, without changing map coverage. Source `F_ZLEV`/`T_ZLEV` topology and endpoint elevations keep Commonwealth Avenue, Beacon Street, and Ipswich Street below the overpass. [MassDOT’s Bowker project](https://www.mass.gov/bowker-overpass-project) confirms the real crossing. These are the retained GIS alignment, not a reconstruction of current construction staging or the future replacement design. The 6.4-meter local-datum main deck and 0.75-meter slab thickness are explicit game estimates, with smooth ramps meeting the existing terrain; they are not measured bridge engineering. Rendered top/underside geometry, Rapier collision, and road-aware traffic heights share the same profile. Supports avoid mapped lower streets. Other grade-separated roads, interstate/freeway classes, expressways/turnpikes, Storrow Drive, and tunnels remain outside this driving iteration; river crossings to Cambridge are not added.
- Roads use authentic centerline paths. Surface widths are prototype assumptions in meters: Boylston 13, Newbury 10, Commonwealth carriageways 9, Marlborough 9, Beacon 12, Dartmouth 11, Clarendon 11, Berkeley 12, Exeter 10, Huntington 15, Saint James 12, Stuart 13, Arlington 14, Columbus 12, Tremont 12, Parker 9, Parker Hill 8, Francis/Longwood 12, Calumet/Fisher/Hillside 8, Saint Alphonsus 9; alleys 4.2 and other local streets 7. Source one-way flags are retained, but traffic behavior and striping are simplified.
- Boylston lane configuration and Copley Square landscaping have changed since the source surveys. The scene is a driving prototype, not a current transportation or navigation product.

## Neighborhood architecture and Fenway Park

Mission Hill's triple-decker treatment is an **inferred architectural profile**, not an address-level inventory. The [Boston Planning Mission Hill overview](https://www.bostonplans.org/neighborhoods/mission-hill/at-a-glance) and [City of Boston Mission Hill Triangle description](https://content.boston.gov/historic-district/mission-hill-triangle-architectural-conservation-district) provide neighborhood context. Runtime selection in `src/render/neighborhood-profiles.ts` uses the official Mission Hill polygon, measured source height of 8.3–14.4 m, footprint area of 65–245 m², separation from adjoining buildings, and frontage toward selected residential streets. Churches, schools, hospitals, and named landmarks are excluded. In the current package, 206 source masses qualify. This is a conservative game-art heuristic; those addresses have not been individually verified as wood-framed three-family buildings.

The selected houses keep their source positions, footprints, bay projections, roofs, and relative massing. Five original clapboard palettes, three-floor window spacing, projecting trim, cornices, and stacked porches convey the local housing type. Window/porch placement is checked against source wall geometry and available street setback; this validates fit, not historical architectural accuracy. Generic masonry storefront details and roof clutter are suppressed for these candidates. Stair runs solve against terrain at their actual lower landing, with ground-reaching masonry and porch footings. Tight front setbacks use side entries with matching railing openings; sites without room for a plausible flight omit the added procedural porch. Decorative steps and rails are not separately surveyed or fully collision-modeled.

Fenway's main shell is Boston model `BOS-73AP7-T4BL0-O4QDM` (catalog name `24 JERSEY ST`), with source stadium components including its 2003 expansion. It already has an open field; no generic roof is substituted. `src/data/fenway-detail.json` adds an **ODbL-derived OpenStreetMap extract**, © OpenStreetMap contributors, obtained from the [official OSM map API](https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/map?bbox=-71.1002,42.3452,-71.0945,42.3484). The stored features are ways 813683133 (field), 813683131 (inner infield), 1339130771 (Green Monster), 813683146 (Monster seats), 148755799 and 148755801 (bullpens), 813683134 (bleachers), and 813683142, 813683143, 813683144, and 814360194 (stands). Mapped markers locate the right-field foul pole and center-field scoreboard. Source IDs, request URL, and interpretive limitations are bundled with the derived coordinates; [OSM attribution and license](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) apply.

The [Red Sox official Fenway facts and figures](https://www.mlb.com/redsox/ballpark/facts-figures) provide the short 310-foot left-field and 302-foot right-field dimensions and Green Monster height. The field layout solves home plate against the mapped foul-pole positions, then constructs a 90-foot basepath square and 60-foot-6-inch pitching distance. Original warning-track, clay, grass, seat, wall, scoreboard, light-bank, and sign geometry completes the landmark. Seat height follows the highest supporting surface across all four source stadium components, including the separately modeled lower bowl; shallow concrete row treads distinguish seating from retained roof surfaces. Street-facing gate/window bays are fitted to measured wall planes. The decorative seating arrangement, red seat, lettering, and scoreboard artwork are interpretations, not a current seat-by-seat survey or live baseball information. No MLB photographs, textures, or third-party stadium model are bundled. Fenway’s main shell uses its original source triangles for collision, preserving the open field. The generated field, seats, and stand details remain decorative and are not a fully playable or collision-modeled stadium interior.

To regenerate the derived Fenway geometry after a city-model rebuild:

```sh
.venv-data/bin/python scripts/build-fenway-detail.py --download
```

This requires the same pinned `pyproj` dependency as the city compiler and the generated `public/data/city.glb`. `--source PATH` reads a previously downloaded OSM XML extract; the default raw cache is ignored `data-source-cache/fenway-osm.xml`. The output is `src/data/fenway-detail.json`, including source roof triangles used to seat the decorative stands.

The three complex buildings using exact mesh collision (Fenway Park, the Prudential complex, and 145 Dartmouth) also use source-edge foundations. Only near-horizontal bottom edges of vertical source walls within 3 cm of the model base are extended to the sampled terrain, following the real concave edge geometry. The visible strips and static collision share identical triangles. No convex skirt or horizontal cap is added, so real street openings, the stadium field, and higher overhangs remain open. These foundations reconcile the model/terrain datums; they are not measured construction details.

## Original environment and ambient characters

Tree species, individual tree placement, pedestrians, and ground-cover details are **original procedural art**, not a municipal vegetation or population inventory. Maple, elm, honey-locust, and white-pine silhouettes use generated leaf masks and branching geometry. Street planting uses broadleaf variants; park planting may include pine. The same branch/leaf resources are shared across instances, with three distance levels and a bounded nearby active field. Wind deformation also applies to the foliage shadow pass. No downloaded leaf photographs or tree models are used.

Park grass combines generated blade/color and relief textures with broad spatial color variation and a capped set of nearby geometric tufts. Tufts are constrained to selected mapped park polygons and excluded from mapped paths, road margins, building footprints, and water. These procedural lawn treatments do not claim actual mowing patterns or plant coverage. Small reflective puddles are placed along suitable near-level road edges; their outlines and positions are fictional, they use the scene's environment reflections, and there is no rain, drainage, or planar-reflection simulation.

Pedestrians use original articulated instanced meshes with varied skin/hair/clothing colors, clothing shapes, bags, and shoes. A bounded pool of up to 112 actors walks clearance-checked sidewalk stretches, targeting 72 nearby walkers in busy commercial districts, 44 in mixed districts, and 26 in residential districts, and shifts distant actors toward the active neighborhood; each route remains grounded by the same terrain query as the scene. They pause near a moving player car, but do not simulate real footfall, road crossing, destinations, or a full crowd-navigation system. The prototype remains free roaming: there are no delivery, checkpoint, or scoring missions.

## Coordinates and conversion

The city models use Boston-shifted Massachusetts State Plane feet (NAD83 / EPSG:2249). Full State Plane coordinates are local model coordinates plus X=731,100 feet, Y=2,902,900 feet. The game adds a second origin shift to keep floating-point coordinates small:

```
world X = (model east feet − 40,000) × (1200 / 3937)
world Z = −(model north feet − 50,000) × (1200 / 3937)
source elevation meters = model elevation feet × (1200 / 3937)
world Y = max(0, source elevation meters − normalizationGroundMeters)
          + building.baseElevation
```

Positive X points east, negative Z points north, and positive Y points up. GIS coordinates are transformed to EPSG:2249 before these offsets are applied. Road geometry is clipped to curated coverage; building meshes with centroids inside it may extend a little beyond the outline. New-area normalization uses the greater of the metre-scale catalog ground and the primary model's minimum source elevation; supported upper components inherit that primary model's normalization and placement. The original Back Bay compatibility branch preserves its prior feet interpretation. `height` remains the model's highest point above its shared base. **GLB Y already includes `baseElevation`; the runtime must not translate it a second time.**

The terrain grid is `{width:955,height:722,cellSize:6,originX:-3408,originZ:-1620,heights:[...]}`. Samples are row-major, with X increasing across a row and world Z increasing down the rows. The last sample is at `(2316,2706)`. Heights are meters relative to the DEM elevation at the original Boylston spawn: **3.385349780189643 meters** at world `(-291.674,36.660)`. The package range is −13.221 to +63.549 meters. This local zero is not a claim that all Back Bay streets are level. Rendering and physics use the same NE–SW cell diagonal and piecewise-linear height query. Before creating the world, `src/simulation/road-grading.ts` derives a separate runtime grid from the bundled DEM: street centerline profiles are smoothed, connected intersections share heights, and paved road widths are leveled with short shoulders blending into the adjoining terrain. Source elevations at building centers and footprint edges limit road fill, while foundation skirts connect modest terrain cuts beneath the fixed models. The correction runs once during loading and retains the existing terrain triangle count. This removes bare-earth bank and depression artifacts from the driving surface while retaining broad uphill/downhill changes. The original packaged elevation samples and building meshes are not rewritten. This is procedural roadbed construction, not a surveyed road-elevation dataset; bridge decks remain separate levels.

## Rebuild

Python 3.11+ with the pinned `pyproj`, Pillow, and Shapely dependencies is sufficient. Generated GLB uses standard glTF 2.0 geometry, flat normals, initial six-meter façade UV repetition, and separate `facade`/`roof` material slots. `buildingId` metadata connects GLB nodes with the JSON map footprints. At load time, wall UV projection is stabilized across nearby triangle orientations, with window proportions defined by the assigned procedural architectural family; source horizontal geometry and relative massing are retained.

```sh
python3 -m venv .venv-data
.venv-data/bin/pip install -r scripts/requirements.txt
.venv-data/bin/python scripts/build-city.py --download --region central
python3 scripts/validate-city.py
```

The download step writes original archives, paginated GIS, a float32 TIFF, elevation JSON, and source requests to the ignored `data-source-cache/` folder. To use an existing cache, pass `--source-dir PATH`. `scripts/fetch-city-sources.py`, `scripts/fetch-terrain.py`, and `scripts/fetch-park-sources.py` can also run separately. The central compiler automatically applies `scripts/build-road-structures.py` to restore the checked-in Bowker alignment/profile layer; that script can also be run independently with `.venv-data/bin/python scripts/build-road-structures.py`. `public/data/road-structures-source.json` preserves the selected raw GIS features, and `provenance.json.roadStructures` records the assumptions and source hash. The central download invokes the park downloader with `--osm-api`; it reuses a cached raw OSM extract when one exists. An optional `--ground-dir PATH` accepts separately cached street layers; both raw ArcGIS JSON and `{ "response": ... }` wrappers are accepted. `--region missionhill` rebuilds the earlier hill corridor, and `--region backbay` rebuilds the original flat area. For safe iteration, compile with `--output-dir /tmp/boston-staging` and validate with `python3 scripts/validate-city.py --data-dir /tmp/boston-staging` before replacing runtime assets.

Validation checks GLB buffers and building vertical placement, ground contact for all **3,524** nonlegacy primary models, **75** independently supported upper components, all **14** destinations, the nine official neighborhood names, the three major park outlines, finite grid coverage, shaped road clipping, original spawn clearance, safe destination arrivals, and continuous undirected height-aware road connections from Back Bay to every destination. The Mission Hill destination retains its summit marker and a separate `driveStart` on the climb. Validation does not certify legal routing or present-day lane rules. The geographic assets currently occupy approximately **57.8 MB GLB + 8.0 MB city JSON + 1.7 MB provenance** before HTTP compression.

Outputs are `public/data/city.glb`, `public/data/city.json`, and `public/data/provenance.json`. The application can therefore run from a static host without a geographic-data API key or external map calls.

## MBTA corridor and vehicle artwork

`public/data/transit.json` contains an offline MBTA/OSM extract obtained October 27, 2026. The [MBTA V3 API](https://www.mbta.com/developers/v3-api) supplies Route 39 shapes `390178`/`390179`, Green Line E shape identifiers `canonical-8000015`/`canonical-8000018`, and stop names/positions. Route 39 retains both complete directions from Back Bay through Huntington Avenue to the existing map boundary: 5.03/5.11 km with 16 stops each. Route shapes are service alignment data, not surveyed lane centerlines. The Prudential complex (`BOS-KBIA4-2XA04-WNETP`), 145 Dartmouth (`BOS-Q6TUK-T7ZG3-2SJKK`), and Fenway’s main shell (`BOS-73AP7-T4BL0-O4QDM`) use their actual source triangles for collision instead of convex footprint proxies. The first two preserve the Huntington/Dartmouth public street corridors; Fenway preserves its source field opening. Other buildings retain conservative convex collision proxies. Driving, camera obstruction, and helicopter clearance query the exact geometry for these three exceptions.

Green Line surface geometry uses the separate directed [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) tracks, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. Inbound ways are 831542201, 9267575, 832390268, 832390271, and 832390272; outbound ways are 832390273, 832390270, 832390269, 218004436, and 831542200. Each direction is approximately 2.76 km with eight mapped stops, from the Heath Street loop to the actual tunnel portal north of Northeastern. Underground ways 642267568/642267673 are excluded. The rail bed, standard-gauge rails, and overhead wire are original simplified geometry along these alignments; catenary height, pole spacing, and bed dimensions are game-art assumptions. The [Massachusetts MBTA system description](https://www.mass.gov/doc/appendix-b-mbta-system-description/download) documents Green Line overhead power and fleet context.

The white/black/yellow 12-meter bus and silver/green articulated 22.5-meter light-rail vehicle are original procedural interpretations, with destination displays, doors, glazing, mirrors, roof equipment, and lighting. They are not exact replicas of a particular fleet unit. Two buses and two trolleys run at a time; station dwells, speed, signals, and frequency are game settings. They brake for nearby vehicles and the player, but do not simulate passenger boarding, a timetable, MBTA dispatch, or train control. No live service API or imagery is requested while playing. All transit and regular-vehicle modeling in this pass is original code; no Slop repository code or third-party vehicle models were copied.

Regular cars have front/rear 12×6-inch Massachusetts-inspired plates, using the [RMV passenger plate manual](https://www.mass.gov/doc/passenger-plates-manual-0/download) for the red registration, blue state name/slogan, and white background layout. Registrations and artwork are fictional; no plate photograph or actual registration record is used. Vehicle models are unbranded and include no NYC taxi or Waymo designs.

Road traffic has a fixed maximum of 64 simulated cars using shared instanced meshes. The official neighborhood polygons determine game density targets: 48 cars within 650 meters in Back Bay, Downtown, Chinatown, and the Leather District; 32 in Fenway and Longwood; 18 in residential areas. Named 7-meter streets in busy districts can carry traffic, and initial/explicit destination placement favors the visible street ahead. Distant cars relocate outside a protected 260-meter radius, enlarged when flying; nearby cars are preserved. Initial placement reserves 35 meters around the player and 18 meters between cars, and a crowded small network can run below the cap instead of accepting overlapping placements. This is a bounded game population, not measured Boston traffic volume.

To regenerate the transit extract using the same `pyproj`/Shapely environment as the city compiler:

```sh
.venv-data/bin/python scripts/build-transit.py --download
```

Omit `--download` to use the ignored `data-source-cache/transit` snapshots. Route data, source way/shape identifiers, attribution, and modeling limitations are bundled in the generated JSON. The compiler clips routes to current `city.coverage`; it does not expand the playable map.

## Massachusetts State House façade

The State House treatment selects the surveyed complex `BOS-1WKHX-X382F-V5VL1` only. Its source triangles, footprint, datum, roof forms, and existing gold dome are retained. The Bulfinch central block receives original metric red-brick textures; the lateral wings receive pale coursed stone and tall sash windows. A procedural south façade adds an arcaded lower porch, slender fluted white columns, Palladian window groups, cornices, balustrades, a brick pediment, and terrain-supported entrance steps. The palette and architectural arrangement follow the [National Park Service exterior photograph](https://www.nps.gov/places/massachusetts-state-house.htm) (credited there to Arlan Fonseca) and the Massachusetts State House Art Commission's [Italy under the Golden Dome](https://www.mass.gov/doc/italy-under-the-golden-dome/download). Those photographs are reference only and are not bundled in the game. Ornament, window spacing, and entrance furniture are simplified artistic interpretations aligned to the source shell, rather than a measured restoration model.
