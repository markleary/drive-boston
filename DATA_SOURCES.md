# Boston source data: Greater Boston

This prototype uses real Boston and Cambridge building meshes, OpenStreetMap buildings for Somerville and Brookline, and mapped street geometry. Its façades, road widths, lane paint, street furniture, and landmark embellishments are interpretive game art, not a survey-accurate reconstruction.

## Included geography

The curated coverage is approximately **140.5 km²**. It is the union of the City of Boston's official GIS polygons for every neighborhood except **Roslindale, West Roxbury, Hyde Park, and the Harbor Islands** (22 polygons: Allston, Back Bay, Bay Village, Beacon Hill, Brighton, Charlestown, Chinatown, Dorchester, Downtown, East Boston, Fenway, Jamaica Plain, Leather District, Longwood, Mattapan, Mission Hill, North End, Roxbury, South Boston, South Boston Waterfront, South End, and West End) and the City's official surrounding-town polygons for **Cambridge, Somerville, and Brookline**, 25 districts in all. An 18-meter margin around the outer edge keeps boundary streets and junctions whole, and the original central-Boston coverage (Mission Hill through Chinatown) is contained unchanged. The coverage is three polygons: the mainland, East Boston across the harbor, and the Long Wharf/Thompson Island fringe. There are no district loading seams; the runtime streams 1 km tiles instead. Fenway is the official GIS label for the neighborhood containing Kenmore Square; the map includes a separate Kenmore Square destination.

The naming authority is the [City of Boston basemap Neighborhoods layer 16](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/16) together with the [surrounding-town layer 17](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/17) of the same service, retrieved October 27 and 28, 2026; `city.neighborhoods` marks the three towns with `town: true`. Its polygons place Boston Common and the Public Garden within Beacon Hill. The City's [Boston Common](https://www.boston.gov/parks/boston-common) and [Public Garden](https://www.boston.gov/parks/public-garden) pages instead use the neighborhood label Downtown. The game consistently uses the GIS polygons for neighborhood lookup and preserves both parks as individually named destinations; neither park is omitted because of naming differences.

The study envelope is west −71.1829, south 42.2589, east −70.9823, north 42.4221. `city.coverage` records the outer playable outlines (exteriors only; the park and water holes stay in `city.neighborhoods`, which records the official polygons). The larger terrain rectangle is not a claim of complete building coverage. The package contains **117,598 buildings, 20,149 road paths, 44,975 curb paths, 1,738 signal locations, 3,168 park polygon parts, 306 merged water polygons, and 7,050 park-path segments**. A park can contain multiple polygon parts, so 3,168 is not a count of separate parks. All **3,763** building IDs from the previous central-Boston build remain included with their earlier vertical normalization. All 47 mainland destinations have a connected surface-road route from the original Back Bay spawn; Maverick Square and Logan Airport in East Boston are marked `detached` because the harbor tunnels are not modeled.

- **Boston buildings:** [Boston Planning 3D downloads](https://www.bostonplans.org/3d-data-maps/3d-smart-model/3d-data-download), June 2026 release. The download page dedicates these downloads under the Open Data Commons **PDDL**. The metro build reads every model tile intersecting the coverage (96 archives; 68 contribute models) and keeps the 76,807 `Current`, `Complete`, and `Approved Demo` models whose centroids fall inside coverage. The exact source URL pattern is `https://maps.bostonplans.org/3d/Bos3d_BldgModels_20260624_OBJ/BOS_G_4_BldgModels_OBJ.zip` (substitute the tile ID).
- **Cambridge buildings:** [Cambridge GIS 3D data](https://www.cambridgema.gov/GIS/3D/3ddata), the 2023 OBJ model tiles C-1 through H-3 (16 tiles, 16,889 models) downloaded from the city's `camb3d` storage. City of Cambridge open data; the catalog's ground elevations are feet, interpreted as such, and no textures are used.
- **Somerville and Brookline buildings, and Cambridge gaps:** [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) building footprints, © OpenStreetMap contributors, **ODbL 1.0**, retrieved October 28, 2026 through the Overpass API for the three town polygons. 23,902 footprints are extruded: 1,558 by `building:levels`, 2 by a `height` tag, and 22,342 by a documented per-type storey default (houses 2.5 floors, apartments 4, offices 4, garages and sheds 1, and so on); house types receive gabled roofs. These are footprint extrusions, not surveyed massing. In Cambridge they fill only blocks the city survey omits.
- **Street centerlines:** [Boston Infrastructure/OpenData layer 8](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/8) for the 14,981 Boston paths. Original geometry, names, and one-way direction flags are retained; vertices closer than half a metre to their neighbour are dropped and a polyline that doubles back on itself is cut, which removes 30 degenerate stubs across both sources (mostly fragments clipped at the town line whose two ends snap to the same Boston endpoint). [Boston's street-segments metadata](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=8f31b239f40847e29543322093c717fc) declares PDDL. The three towns use 5,168 OpenStreetMap highway ways from `trunk` to `living_street` (service, pedestrian, and motorway classes omitted) with widths by class; 113 way ends are snapped onto Boston centerline ends at the city line so the networks connect.
- **Curbs:** [Boston Infrastructure/OpenData layer 6](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/6) inside Boston; the towns get 5,037 synthetic curb paths offset from their street centerlines, a game construction rather than mapped curbs.
- **Signal locations:** [Boston Infrastructure/OpenData layer 12](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/12) inside Boston, and OpenStreetMap `highway=traffic_signals` nodes in the towns.
- **Terrain:** [USGS 3DEP Bare Earth DEM ImageServer](https://elevation.nationalmap.gov/arcgis/rest/services/3DEPElevation/ImageServer), exported October 28, 2026 for the whole envelope. The source supplies numeric elevation rather than a hillshade picture. [USGS 3DEP products](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program) are available without use restrictions. The raw float32 TIFF, full export request, grid parameters, vertical reference, and source hash are recorded by the preprocessing scripts; provenance is bundled with the game.
- **Neighborhood and town polygons:** [Boston basemap layer 16](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/16) and [layer 17](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/17), using the `name` field and complete official polygon rings.
- **Parks:** [Boston basemap layer 14](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/14), using `SITE_NAME` and `TypeLong`, which also covers the three towns, supplemented by OpenStreetMap park, garden, and cemetery polygons there. Park geometry is clipped to coverage; existing Copley Square art remains separately authored.
- **Water:** [Boston basemap Hydro polygons layer 12](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/12), supplemented by OpenStreetMap water polygons. Overlapping outlines are geometrically united before packaging to avoid drawing two competing water surfaces. The Charles River basin, the Mystic, the Neponset, and the harbor are clipped to the terrain envelope, so water remains visible immediately beyond the developed area.
- **Park paths and additional water detail:** [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), © OpenStreetMap contributors, **ODbL 1.0**. Extracts were retrieved October 27 and 28, 2026 from the official OSM API and the Overpass API for every mapped park. The raw source, actual request boxes, and method are cached. Footway/path/pedestrian/step centerlines are clipped to official park polygons; tagged bridge segments retain that attribute. Path widths are source tags where present and otherwise documented game assumptions. No OSM map tiles or photographs are bundled. The derived open geographic data and source attribution remain available in the static package and preprocessing scripts.

- **Road inventory inspected, not used as current authoritative lane data:** [layer 14](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/14). Some fields are blank, zero, or carry old dates. The prototype therefore uses documented width assumptions instead of presenting them as current lane measurements.

Street GIS requests use the selected envelope, `inSR=4326`, `outSR=4326`, `outFields=*`, and `spatialRel=esriSpatialRelIntersects`. Object IDs are fetched first and features downloaded in batches to avoid silent record-limit truncation. Raw data is cached for preprocessing rather than requested by each player. Attribution is retained in the application's credits and in `public/data/city.json`.

## Deliberate limitations

- The June 2026 publication date is **not** a fresh survey of every building. Most selected building shapes originate in 2011 observations. Per-model source survey dates, methods, status, and tile IDs are retained in `public/data/provenance.json`.
- A mesh can span several attached addresses or represent one building part. It is not necessarily one parcel or one address.
- `Current`, `Complete`, and `Approved Demo` buildings are included. An approved demolition is still standing according to the catalog. Historical, merely approved, and under-construction models are omitted. Low ancillary walls are omitted. This avoids drawing historical/proposed structures over existing buildings.
- Two survey meshes are structures rather than buildings: the Zakim Bridge model `BOS-2E9SM-83L3W-UVT08`, which includes the Charlestown I-93 viaduct, is kept for its skyline and driven under with exact triangle collision, no foundation skirt, no far-distance box, and a token 6 m footprint, so its 2 km convex hull never blocks streets, sidewalks, the helicopter, or the TD Garden arrival. The Logan terminal complex `BOS-4WO5X-6OJI5-G5S26` keeps its footprint but also uses exact collision, since its hull would close the terminal road loop.
- Downloaded OBJ exports contain no photographic façade textures. Runtime façades are original procedural materials. No Google, Nearmap, or Mapillary photographs are bundled.
- Façade selection does not analyze satellite imagery. Reviewed survey-ID identities and religious building/amenity tags take precedence over name cues, followed by height/footprint heuristics. `src/data/building-identities.json` supplies the same identity and name rules to the renderer and Python compiler. Numeric Church/Temple Street addresses and explicitly named housing are not religious-use evidence. Missing semantic metadata can still require a reviewed identity correction.
- **Mission Church correction:** Boston's F6 catalog has two `Current` meshes for the Basilica of Our Lady of Perpetual Help: `BOS-ADQVO-277LT-NQB6N` (`1545 TREMONT ST`, 2021 survey) and `BOS-U59XZ-OVMVF-D1IT5` (`1545 -1565 TREMONT ST`, 2011 survey), both on parcel `1000012000`, with blank `StructUse`. The old church/chapel/cathedral name check missed these address-only names; their 70.79/71.57 m spire heights triggered the >65 m office/glass fallback. Both IDs now have an explicit church identity (original names retained as `sourceName`), consistent distant masonry, and a dedicated stone/round-arch/rose-window material treatment guided by the user-provided aerial and street photographs. Material partitions retain all surveyed positions and normals, including the overlapping catalog shells; the photos are references, not bundled texture assets.
- Storefront glazing, signs, and awnings are original interpretive art. Their labels are fictional, and their street-facing placement is checked against the real building mesh; they do not reproduce actual businesses.
- Roof-mounted air-conditioning units and fans are original interpretive props. Their full clearance area is checked against flat source roof triangles; they do not claim to reproduce actual rooftop installations.
- Projecting window trim, cornices, stoops, and railings are original game geometry attached to compatible source surfaces. The window grid follows the procedural material coordinates; these details are not individual building surveys.
- Copley Square landscaping interprets the [current Boston Parks illustrative site plan](https://www.boston.gov/departments/parks-and-recreation/project/copley-square-park-improvements), reviewed October 26, 2026. Raised grove, fountain-side groves, plaza, small Trinity-side lawn, and seating are approximate original meshes aligned to the surrounding road grid. No plan images are bundled, and this is not a measured as-built landscape model.
- Player/traffic models, pedestrian figures, foliage, and props are original procedural art. Parking placement and pedestrian routes are checked for road/building clearance; they do not claim current legal parking rules or measured footfall.
- Sound effects were generated with ElevenLabs, not captured in Boston. Prompts, durations, model ID, and asset hashes are retained in `public/audio/provenance.json`; the API key is used only by the local generation script.
- The title screen's background, `public/title-scene.jpg`, is an in-game screenshot of this prototype, not a photograph. Its Bebas Neue, Barlow, and JetBrains Mono typefaces are bundled from the `@fontsource` npm packages under the SIL Open Font License 1.1; no font service is contacted at runtime.
- Hills come from the **actual bare-earth DEM**, packaged at 6-meter spacing. Bilinear export resampling and a gentle 3×3 `[1,2,1]` separable smoothing pass suppress small retaining-wall/kerb aliasing; these are not centimeter-accurate road surfaces or constructed ramps. The terrain ranges from about 80 meters below the Back Bay starting datum (the harbor floor) to about 111 meters above it in Brighton and Brookline. The Parker Hill Avenue destination is approximately 58.82 meters above the datum.
- Street levelling (`scripts/grade-terrain.mjs`) cannot build a retaining wall. Where the source centerlines leave two streets at grade within a few metres of each other while the DEM puts one of them several metres lower (the South Boston Haul Road in its cut beside the Dorchester Avenue bridge abutments), the pavements meet through a short bank instead of a wall: the Haul Road keeps its underpass beneath the Dorchester Avenue deck and carries an internal bank of about 8% in the grid cell it shares with the abutment, down from 39% before the landings were held inside the solver. Approach embankments that the sources carry no level for (the Cambridge end of the BU Bridge and the Memorial Drive ramps, Mountfort Street and Saint Mary's Street beside the Turnpike cut) keep a bank across part of their assumed pavement width where the DEM crest is narrower than the street; First Street in Cambridge is down to about 6% and Frontage Road on the Expressway fill is level. `tests/road-grade-coverage.test.mjs` counts the grade separations, holds the banked sections to a short list with a drivable ceiling, and audits the rest of the network against the original crossfall limits.
- Buildings retain source horizontal placement and relative massing. An audit found that the catalog's historical `Gnd_El_Ft` field contains metre-scale ground estimates, while OBJ coordinates are feet. New-area primary models use metre-correct ground normalization, with their actual source floor seated at the highest DEM sample around the footprint minus 0.1 meter. Runtime foundation skirts close the downhill gap. This is a practical terrain seating approximation, not an as-built foundation survey; large sloping sites may have visible retaining bases.
- **Supported upper parts remain elevated:** source components that sit on another model share that support model's translation, rather than being individually dropped to the street. Their `partOf` relationship requires at least 90% footprint support and compatible original vertical extents. The validator independently checks both. Low ancillary meshes are omitted. Three additional central-city source files contain datum/helper vertices or collapsed underground triangles; normalization uses the lowest surviving visible face to prevent these helpers from leaving the building suspended above its base.
- The original 164 Back Bay models retain their legacy vertical normalization so the already authored landmark wall planes and silhouettes remain unchanged. This compatibility policy, the catalogue value, selected normalization ground in meters, original source min/max elevations, and any ground-contact offset are recorded per model in `public/data/provenance.json`.
- **Selected Bowker/Charlesgate structures are now independent upper roadways:** 30 retained City GIS centerline segments restore the Bowker carriageways/ramps and connecting Boylston bridge approaches, with two additional existing Commonwealth Avenue paths replaced by lower bridge decks across the Muddy River, without changing map coverage. Source `F_ZLEV`/`T_ZLEV` topology and endpoint elevations keep Commonwealth Avenue, Beacon Street, and Ipswich Street below the overpass. [MassDOT’s Bowker project](https://www.mass.gov/bowker-overpass-project) confirms the real crossing. These are the retained GIS alignment, not a reconstruction of current construction staging or the future replacement design. The 6.4-meter local-datum main deck and 0.75-meter slab thickness are explicit game estimates, with smooth ramps meeting the existing terrain; they are not measured bridge engineering. Rendered top/underside geometry, Rapier collision, and road-aware traffic heights share the same profile. Supports avoid mapped lower streets. Elsewhere in the region, 540 road segments whose source level topology or water crossing marks them as bridges receive the same generic 6.4 m deck profile with smooth approach ramps, which connects Cambridge, Charlestown, and the Seaport across the Charles, the Mystic, and Fort Point Channel; these are estimated profiles on the real alignments, not surveyed decks. Interstate/freeway classes, expressways/turnpikes, Storrow Drive, the Central Artery, and tunnels remain outside the street network (34 Boston centerline IDs are omitted for that reason), so East Boston is reached only by helicopter.
- Roads use authentic centerline paths. Surface widths are prototype assumptions in meters: Boylston 13, Newbury 10, Commonwealth carriageways 9, Marlborough 9, Beacon 12, Dartmouth 11, Clarendon 11, Berkeley 12, Exeter 10, Huntington 15, Saint James 12, Stuart 13, Arlington 14, Columbus 12, Tremont 12, Parker 9, Parker Hill 8, Francis/Longwood 12, Calumet/Fisher/Hillside 8, Saint Alphonsus 9; other Boston streets by their functional class (arterials 14 and 12, collectors 10, local streets 7); alleys 4.2; OpenStreetMap trunk 14, primary 13, secondary 11, tertiary 9, residential 7, living streets 6. Source one-way flags are retained, but traffic behavior and striping are simplified.
- Boylston lane configuration and Copley Square landscaping have changed since the source surveys. The scene is a driving prototype, not a current transportation or navigation product.

## Neighborhood architecture and Fenway Park

Mission Hill's triple-decker treatment is an **inferred architectural profile**, not an address-level inventory. The [Boston Planning Mission Hill overview](https://www.bostonplans.org/neighborhoods/mission-hill/at-a-glance) and [City of Boston Mission Hill Triangle description](https://content.boston.gov/historic-district/mission-hill-triangle-architectural-conservation-district) provide neighborhood context. Runtime selection in `src/render/neighborhood-profiles.ts` uses the official Mission Hill polygon, measured source height of 8.3–14.4 m, footprint area of 65–245 m², separation from adjoining buildings, and frontage toward selected residential streets. Churches, schools, hospitals, and named landmarks are excluded. In the current package, 206 source masses qualify. This is a conservative game-art heuristic; those addresses have not been individually verified as wood-framed three-family buildings.

The selected houses keep their source positions, footprints, bay projections, roofs, and relative massing. Five original clapboard palettes, three-floor window spacing, projecting trim, cornices, and stacked porches convey the local housing type. Window/porch placement is checked against source wall geometry and available street setback; this validates fit, not historical architectural accuracy. Generic masonry storefront details and roof clutter are suppressed for these candidates. Stair runs solve against terrain at their actual lower landing, with ground-reaching masonry and porch footings. Tight front setbacks use side entries with matching railing openings; sites without room for a plausible flight omit the added procedural porch. Decorative steps and rails are not separately surveyed or fully collision-modeled.

Fenway's main shell is Boston model `BOS-73AP7-T4BL0-O4QDM` (catalog name `24 JERSEY ST`), with source stadium components including its 2003 expansion. It already has an open field; no generic roof is substituted. `src/data/fenway-detail.json` adds an **ODbL-derived OpenStreetMap extract**, © OpenStreetMap contributors, obtained from the [official OSM map API](https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/map?bbox=-71.1002,42.3452,-71.0945,42.3484). The stored features are ways 813683133 (field), 813683131 (inner infield), 1339130771 (Green Monster), 813683146 (Monster seats), 148755799 and 148755801 (bullpens), 813683134 (bleachers), and 813683142, 813683143, 813683144, and 814360194 (stands). Mapped markers locate the right-field foul pole and center-field scoreboard. Source IDs, request URL, and interpretive limitations are bundled with the derived coordinates; [OSM attribution and license](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) apply.

The [Red Sox official Fenway facts and figures](https://www.mlb.com/redsox/ballpark/facts-figures) provide the short 310-foot left-field and 302-foot right-field dimensions and Green Monster height. The field layout solves home plate against the mapped foul-pole positions, then constructs a 90-foot basepath square and 60-foot-6-inch pitching distance. Original warning-track, clay, grass, seat, wall, scoreboard, light-bank, and sign geometry completes the landmark. Seat height follows the highest supporting surface across all four source stadium components, including the separately modeled lower bowl; shallow concrete row treads distinguish seating from retained roof surfaces. Street-facing gate/window bays are fitted to measured wall planes. The decorative seating arrangement, red seat, lettering, and scoreboard artwork are interpretations, not a current seat-by-seat survey or live baseball information. No MLB photographs, textures, or third-party stadium model are bundled. Fenway’s main shell uses its original source triangles for collision, preserving the open field. The generated field, seats, and stand details remain decorative and are not a fully playable or collision-modeled stadium interior.

To regenerate the derived Fenway geometry after a city-model rebuild:

```sh
.venv-data/bin/python scripts/build-fenway-detail.py --download
```

This requires the same pinned `pyproj` dependency as the city compiler and the installed `public/data` tiles, from which it reads the four stadium components' roof triangles (quantized to 1 cm). `--source PATH` reads a previously downloaded OSM XML extract; the default raw cache is ignored `data-source-cache/fenway-osm.xml`. The output is `src/data/fenway-detail.json`, including source roof triangles used to seat the decorative stands.

The complex buildings using exact mesh collision (Fenway Park, the Prudential complex, 145 Dartmouth, and the Logan terminals) also use source-edge foundations; the Zakim Bridge structure uses exact collision without one. Only near-horizontal bottom edges of vertical source walls within 3 cm of the model base are extended to the sampled terrain, following the real concave edge geometry. The visible strips and static collision share identical triangles. No convex skirt or horizontal cap is added, so real street openings, the stadium field, and higher overhangs remain open. These foundations reconcile the model/terrain datums; they are not measured construction details.

## Original environment and ambient characters

Tree species, individual tree placement, pedestrians, and ground-cover details are **original procedural art**, not a municipal vegetation or population inventory. Maple, elm, honey-locust, and white-pine silhouettes use generated leaf masks and branching geometry. Street planting uses broadleaf variants; park planting may include pine. The same branch/leaf resources are shared across instances, with three distance levels and a bounded nearby active field. Wind deformation also applies to the foliage shadow pass. No downloaded leaf photographs or tree models are used.

Park grass combines generated blade/color and relief textures with broad spatial color variation and a capped set of nearby geometric tufts. Tufts are constrained to selected mapped park polygons and excluded from mapped paths, road margins, building footprints, and water. These procedural lawn treatments do not claim actual mowing patterns or plant coverage. Small reflective puddles are placed along suitable near-level road edges; their outlines and positions are fictional, they use the scene's environment reflections, and there is no rain, drainage, or planar-reflection simulation.

Pedestrians use original articulated instanced meshes with varied skin/hair/clothing colors, clothing shapes, bags, and shoes. A bounded pool of up to 112 actors walks clearance-checked sidewalk stretches, targeting 72 nearby walkers in busy commercial districts, 44 in mixed districts, and 26 in residential districts, and shifts distant actors toward the active neighborhood; each route remains grounded by the same terrain query as the scene. They pause near a moving player car, but do not simulate real footfall, road crossing, destinations, or a full crowd-navigation system. The prototype remains free roaming: there are no delivery, checkpoint, or scoring missions.

## Coordinates and conversion

The city models use Boston-shifted Massachusetts State Plane feet (NAD83 / EPSG:2249). Full State Plane coordinates are local model coordinates plus X=731,100 feet, Y=2,902,900 feet. The game adds a second origin shift to keep floating-point coordinates small:

```
world X = (model east feet − 40,000) × (1200 / 3937)
world Z = −(model north feet − 50,000) × (1200 / 3937)
source elevation meters = model elevation feet × (1200 / 3937)
world Y = max(0, source elevation meters − normalizationGroundMeters)
          + building.baseElevation
```

Positive X points east, negative Z points north, and positive Y points up. GIS coordinates are transformed to EPSG:2249 before these offsets are applied. Road geometry is clipped to curated coverage; building meshes with centroids inside it may extend a little beyond the outline. New-area normalization uses the greater of the metre-scale catalog ground and the primary model's minimum source elevation; supported upper components inherit that primary model's normalization and placement. The original Back Bay compatibility branch preserves its prior feet interpretation. `height` remains the model's highest point above its shared base. **GLB Y already includes `baseElevation`; the runtime must not translate it a second time.**

The terrain grid is `{width:2774,height:3039,cellSize:6,originX:-8946,originZ:-8046}` with its samples in the gzip-compressed int16 centimetre sidecar `elevation.bin` (`city.elevation.file`, `scale: 0.01`, `encoding: "int16-gzip"`). Samples are row-major, with X increasing across a row and world Z increasing down the rows. Heights are meters relative to the DEM elevation at the original Boylston spawn: **3.385349780189643 meters** at world `(-291.674,36.660)`. The package range is about −80.2 to +110.8 meters. This local zero is not a claim that all Back Bay streets are level. Rendering and physics use the same NE–SW cell diagonal and piecewise-linear height query. `src/simulation/road-grading.ts` derives the drivable grid from the DEM: street centerline profiles are smoothed, connected intersections share heights, and paved road widths are leveled with short shoulders blending into the adjoining terrain. The ground-level end of every bridge deck is a hard datum: the terrain triangle under each landing is held at the deck's surveyed height inside every constraint pass (the landing point alone, because neighbouring sub-10 m deck pieces land at different heights and pinning whole lines lets one small cell absorb the conflict), so approach streets climb to the abutment instead of being levelled into the lower road and forced back up at the end. Source elevations at building centers and footprint edges bound road fill and may lower pavement vertices, but never more than 3 m below the source ground; foundation skirts connect such modest terrain cuts beneath the fixed models. For the metro package that correction is run offline by `scripts/grade-terrain.mjs` (the same code, on 90 overlapping 2 km windows in worker threads); `elevation.bin` ships the graded grid, `elevation-source.bin` the untouched DEM export, and `city.elevation.grading` records the road count, changed cells, and maximum cut and fill. Holding the landings inside the solver took the Dorchester Avenue abutment beside the South Boston Haul Road from a 22% crossfall and a 39% internal bank to 3.6% and 7.9%, and cut the number of 2 m road sections banked 6% or more across their full width from 194 to 17 metro-wide. This removes bare-earth bank and depression artifacts from the driving surface while retaining broad uphill/downhill changes. The building meshes are not rewritten. This is procedural roadbed construction, not a surveyed road-elevation dataset; bridge decks remain separate levels and meet the graded ground within a centimetre at their landings.

## Rebuild

Python 3.11+ with the pinned `pyproj`, NumPy, and Shapely dependencies and Node.js 24 are sufficient. The package is a city index plus binary sidecars:

- `city.json`: origin, bounds, coverage outlines, spawn, sources, the elevation and tile manifests, every road, signal, park, water body, neighborhood, and destination, and `landmarkBuildings`, the buildings the landmark modules address before their tiles load. It holds no building list of its own.
- `tiles/t_{tx}_{tz}.bin` (DBT2, 191 files): one gzip tile per 1 km square containing the buildings whose centers fall in it (metadata plus their quantized shells: int16 positions at 1 cm around a per-building anchor, or a coarser `scale` of 2, 5, or 10 cm when a very large mesh would overflow int16; the Prudential and the Logan terminal complex use 2 cm and the Zakim Bridge 5 cm), the curbs, park paths, and road IDs in the square. `scripts/tileformat.py` writes and `src/simulation/tile-format.ts` reads the same layout; facade and roof triangles are split by normal and separate vertices are deduplicated.
- `far.bin` (DBF2): every building's convex footprint at decimetre precision with height, base, facade family, and flags (landmark, exact-mesh, hidden, massing fragment), for the far-distance boxes, the helicopter's obstacle index, sidewalk clearance, and building lookup before a tile loads. Sprawling complexes over 60 m tall with more than 15,000 m² of hull, or any hull over 100,000 m², are hidden and followed by terraced fragments (connected 20 m cells above 10, 25, 50, and 75% of the height) so a podium with towers does not draw as one slab.
- `elevation.bin` and `elevation-source.bin`: the graded and raw DEM grids described above.
- `provenance.json`: sources, per-model normalization policies, tile and grading parameters, omitted road IDs, and the Bowker structure assumptions.

At load time, wall UV projection is stabilized across nearby triangle orientations, with window proportions defined by the assigned procedural architectural family; source horizontal geometry and relative massing are retained.

```sh
python3 -m venv .venv-data
.venv-data/bin/pip install -r scripts/requirements.txt
.venv-data/bin/python scripts/fetch-metro-sources.py --source-dir data-source-cache
.venv-data/bin/python scripts/build-metro.py --output-dir /tmp/boston-staging
node scripts/grade-terrain.mjs --data-dir /tmp/boston-staging
.venv-data/bin/python scripts/build-road-structures.py --data-dir /tmp/boston-staging
.venv-data/bin/python scripts/validate-city.py --data-dir /tmp/boston-staging
```

The fetch step writes the official boundary polygons, paginated GIS layers, the Boston and Cambridge OBJ archives, the Overpass extracts for the towns, and the float32 terrain TIFF with its request to the ignored `data-source-cache/` folder; `build-metro.py --source-dir PATH` reads an existing cache and takes about four minutes. Grading takes about a minute with ten worker threads and keeps the raw grid beside the graded one. `scripts/build-road-structures.py` restores the checked-in Bowker alignment/profile layer against the graded grid; `public/data/road-structures-source.json` preserves the selected raw GIS features, and `provenance.json.roadStructures` records the assumptions and source hash. Copy a validated staging directory over `public/data/` (keeping `transit.json` and `road-structures-source.json`), then regenerate the Fenway detail and transit extracts, which read the installed package. `scripts/build-city.py` still compiles the earlier single-GLB central, Mission Hill, and Back Bay packages, and the runtime keeps that whole-city path for comparison, but `validate-city.py` now checks the tiled metro package only.

Validation parses every tile and the far file and checks the manifest against the files shipped, unique IDs filed in the tile of their center, coverage, normalization policies, finite quantized positions, index ranges, documented base and height per building, at least 90% footprint support for upper parts, ground contact for every nonlegacy primary model, the structure and exact-mesh sets, the retained Back Bay churches and models, the graded and raw grids with the recorded grading statistics and the spawn datum, road widths, one-way flags, coverage, and deck profiles, the absence of limited-access highway names, spawn clearance, all **49** destinations with safe in-roadway arrivals outside buildings, the 25 district names and excluded neighborhoods, the major park outlines, the Mission Hill and Brigham Circle elevations, and continuous undirected height-aware road connections from Back Bay to at least 90% of the network and to every mainland destination. Validation does not certify legal routing or present-day lane rules. The geographic assets occupy approximately **83 MB** before HTTP compression: 54.3 MB of tiles, 11.0 MB graded and 11.0 MB raw elevation, 3.8 MB far hulls, 6.1 MB city JSON, and 9 KB provenance. The raw elevation grid is read only by tests and tools, so a session fetches about 21 MB plus its nearby tiles.

The application can therefore run from a static host without a geographic-data API key or external map calls.

## MBTA corridor and vehicle artwork

`public/data/transit.json` contains an offline MBTA/OSM extract obtained October 27, 2026. The [MBTA V3 API](https://www.mbta.com/developers/v3-api) supplies Route 39 shapes `390178`/`390179`, Green Line E shape identifiers `canonical-8000015`/`canonical-8000018`, and stop names/positions. Route 39 now runs both complete directions from Back Bay through Huntington Avenue and Jamaica Plain to Forest Hills: 8.37/8.12 km with 33 stops each. Route shapes are service alignment data, not surveyed lane centerlines. The Prudential complex (`BOS-KBIA4-2XA04-WNETP`), 145 Dartmouth (`BOS-Q6TUK-T7ZG3-2SJKK`), Fenway’s main shell (`BOS-73AP7-T4BL0-O4QDM`), the Logan terminals (`BOS-4WO5X-6OJI5-G5S26`), and the Zakim Bridge (`BOS-2E9SM-83L3W-UVT08`) use their actual source triangles for collision instead of convex footprint proxies. The first two preserve the Huntington/Dartmouth public street corridors, Fenway preserves its source field opening, Logan its terminal road loop, and the Zakim its underpasses. Other buildings retain conservative convex collision proxies. Driving, camera obstruction, and helicopter clearance query the exact geometry for these exceptions.

Green Line surface geometry uses the separate directed [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) tracks, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. Inbound ways are 831542201, 9267575, 832390268, 832390271, and 832390272; outbound ways are 832390273, 832390270, 832390269, 218004436, and 831542200. Each direction is approximately 2.76 km with eight mapped stops, from the Heath Street loop to the actual tunnel portal north of Northeastern. Underground ways 642267568/642267673 are excluded. The rail bed, standard-gauge rails, and overhead wire are original simplified geometry along these alignments; catenary height, pole spacing, and bed dimensions are game-art assumptions. The [Massachusetts MBTA system description](https://www.mass.gov/doc/appendix-b-mbta-system-description/download) documents Green Line overhead power and fleet context.

The white/black/yellow 12-meter bus and silver/green articulated 22.5-meter light-rail vehicle are original procedural interpretations, with destination displays, doors, glazing, mirrors, roof equipment, and lighting. They are not exact replicas of a particular fleet unit. Two buses and two trolleys run at a time; station dwells, speed, signals, and frequency are game settings. They brake for nearby vehicles and the player, but do not simulate passenger boarding, a timetable, MBTA dispatch, or train control. No live service API or imagery is requested while playing. All transit and regular-vehicle modeling in this pass is original code; no Slop repository code or third-party vehicle models were copied.

Regular cars have front/rear 12×6-inch Massachusetts-inspired plates, using the [RMV passenger plate manual](https://www.mass.gov/doc/passenger-plates-manual-0/download) for the red registration, blue state name/slogan, and white background layout. Registrations and artwork are fictional; no plate photograph or actual registration record is used. Vehicle models are unbranded and include no NYC taxi or Waymo designs.

Road traffic has a fixed maximum of 64 simulated cars using shared instanced meshes. The official neighborhood polygons determine game density targets: 48 cars within 650 meters in Back Bay, Downtown, Chinatown, the Leather District, the North End, the West End, the Seaport, and Bay Village; 32 in Fenway, Longwood, the South End, Charlestown, Allston, East Boston, Cambridge, Somerville, and Brookline; 18 in residential areas. Named 7-meter streets in busy districts can carry traffic, and initial/explicit destination placement favors the visible street ahead. Distant cars relocate outside a protected 260-meter radius, enlarged when flying; nearby cars are preserved. Initial placement reserves 35 meters around the player and 18 meters between cars, and a crowded small network can run below the cap instead of accepting overlapping placements. This is a bounded game population, not measured Boston traffic volume.

To regenerate the transit extract using the same `pyproj`/Shapely environment as the city compiler:

```sh
.venv-data/bin/python scripts/build-transit.py --download
```

Omit `--download` to use the ignored `data-source-cache/transit` snapshots. Route data, source way/shape identifiers, attribution, and modeling limitations are bundled in the generated JSON. The compiler clips routes to current `city.coverage`; it does not expand the playable map. No other MBTA lines are modeled.

## Massachusetts State House façade

The State House treatment selects the surveyed complex `BOS-1WKHX-X382F-V5VL1` only. Its source triangles, footprint, datum, roof forms, and existing gold dome are retained. The Bulfinch central block receives original metric red-brick textures; the lateral wings receive pale coursed stone and tall sash windows. A procedural south façade adds an arcaded lower porch, slender fluted white columns, Palladian window groups, cornices, balustrades, a brick pediment, and terrain-supported entrance steps. The palette and architectural arrangement follow the [National Park Service exterior photograph](https://www.nps.gov/places/massachusetts-state-house.htm) (credited there to Arlan Fonseca) and the Massachusetts State House Art Commission's [Italy under the Golden Dome](https://www.mass.gov/doc/italy-under-the-golden-dome/download). Those photographs are reference only and are not bundled in the game. Ornament, window spacing, and entrance furniture are simplified artistic interpretations aligned to the source shell, rather than a measured restoration model.

## The Embrace, Boston Common

[*The Embrace*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Embrace) (Hank Willis Thomas with MASS Design Group, unveiled January 2023) stands at OpenStreetMap node 10552518542 (42.3549357, −71.0643618), which converts to game position (858.64, −538.98), between the Boston Common Visitor Center and the Parkman Bandstand. The surrounding 1965 Freedom Plaza outline, its inner paving ring, and the curved retaining wall on its uphill east and south edge come from OSM ways 1132661865, 1131759734, and 1334814996, © OpenStreetMap contributors, **ODbL 1.0**, retrieved October 28, 2026 through the Overpass API and converted with the same EPSG:2249 transform as the city package. The plaza drapes on the DEM; park trees, benches, lamps, and close grass stay off it.

No scan or third-party 3D model is bundled. The only public scan found ([HistoryView on Sketchfab](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/none-22b6fa286fb74b11935ce6f66c7879a2)) is not downloadable and carries a standard, non-redistributable license. The bronze is therefore an original procedural interpretation, guided by published photographs and King Boston's design renderings as reference only: four tapered arms (Martin's suit sleeves with buttoned cuffs, Coretta's bare arms with her bracelet), jointed hands, and a front-to-back passage with at least 2.2 m of headroom. It is fitted to 6.1 m (20 ft, the OSM height tag) tall and 7.6 m (25 ft) wide. Arm paths, hand poses, and the yaw, which points the sleeve face toward the Parkman Bandstand, are estimates rather than measurements. A simplified hull of the arms and hands, plus the retaining wall, is added to static collision for driving, camera, and helicopter queries.

## Prudential Tower crown

The crown treatment selects the surveyed Prudential complex `BOS-KBIA4-2XA04-WNETP` and aligns to its tower shaft, measured from the source walls as a 46.6 by 55.3 m rectangle rotated 20.19° (corners (-664.97, 314.34), (-621.20, 298.25), (-602.11, 350.17), and (-645.88, 366.26)). Above the 216.44 m parapet, a dark recessed floor on slender posts, a cantilevered cream slab, a 7.5 m glass band, and a roof slab replace the view of the source setback volume. That volume, 2.3 to 2.8 m inside the shaft faces, is kept but hidden behind the new walls; its 230.25 m roof sits under the new roof slab. Proportions follow photographs of the 1964 tower. The PRUDENTIAL lettering is 2.3 m tall, appears on all four faces, and uses original extruded serif letterforms rather than a font file or the company's artwork. Source rooftop structures above the crown roof keep every source triangle and receive dark ribbed metal cladding instead of office windows. Because the source collision is the setback volume, a solid box around the slab overhangs is added to static collision for driving, camera, and helicopter queries. Glazing patterns, slab thicknesses, and the night lighting are simplified artistic interpretations, not measured details.
