# Boston source data: Greater Boston

This prototype uses real Boston and Cambridge building meshes, OpenStreetMap buildings for Somerville, Brookline, and Chelsea, and mapped street geometry. Its façades, road widths, lane paint, street furniture, and landmark embellishments are interpretive game art, not a survey-accurate reconstruction.

## Included geography

The curated coverage is approximately **148.5 km²**. It is the union of the City of Boston's official GIS polygons for every neighborhood except **Roslindale, West Roxbury, Hyde Park, and the Harbor Islands** (22 polygons: Allston, Back Bay, Bay Village, Beacon Hill, Brighton, Charlestown, Chinatown, Dorchester, Downtown, East Boston, Fenway, Jamaica Plain, Leather District, Longwood, Mattapan, Mission Hill, North End, Roxbury, South Boston, South Boston Waterfront, South End, and West End) and the City's official surrounding-town polygons for **Cambridge, Somerville, Brookline, and Chelsea**, 26 districts in all. An 18-meter margin around the outer edge keeps boundary streets and junctions whole, and the original central-Boston coverage (Mission Hill through Chinatown) is contained unchanged. The runtime coverage has one connected exterior outline linking the mainland, Chelsea, and East Boston through the retained and new bridge corridors. This area is calculated from the playable exterior outline, including enclosed water; the earlier 140.5 km² metadata understated that outline’s area. There are no district loading seams; the runtime streams 1 km tiles instead. Fenway is the official GIS label for the neighborhood containing Kenmore Square; the map includes a separate Kenmore Square destination.

The naming authority is the [City of Boston basemap Neighborhoods layer 16](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/16) together with the [surrounding-town layer 17](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/17) of the same service, retrieved October 27–29, 2026; `city.neighborhoods` marks the four towns with `town: true`. Its polygons place Boston Common and the Public Garden within Beacon Hill. The City's [Boston Common](https://www.boston.gov/parks/boston-common) and [Public Garden](https://www.boston.gov/parks/public-garden) pages instead use the neighborhood label Downtown. The game consistently uses the GIS polygons for neighborhood lookup and preserves both parks as individually named destinations; neither park is omitted because of naming differences.

The study envelope is west −71.1829, south 42.2589, east −70.9823, north 42.4221. `city.coverage` records the outer playable outlines (exteriors only; the park and water holes stay in `city.neighborhoods`, which records the official polygons). The larger terrain rectangle is not a claim of complete building coverage. The package contains **122,213 buildings, 20,708 road paths (20,695 surface/deck paths plus 13 underground bores and ramps), 50,046 curb paths (44,918 surveyed, 5,128 synthetic town curbs), 27,836 surveyed sidewalk polygons in 30,270 tile parts, 20,063 curb-bounded roadway records, 184 concrete curb islands, 7,353 asphalt driveway and parking surfaces in 8,451 tile parts, 1,791 signal locations, 3,276 park polygon parts, 309 merged water polygons, and 7,739 park-path segments**. A park can contain multiple polygon parts, so 3,276 is not a count of separate parks. All **3,763** building IDs from the previous central-Boston build remain included with their earlier vertical normalization. All 59 destinations have a connected surface-road route from the original Back Bay spawn, including Maverick Square and Logan Airport via Chelsea and the Chelsea Creek crossings.

- **Boston buildings:** [Boston Planning 3D downloads](https://www.bostonplans.org/3d-data-maps/3d-smart-model/3d-data-download), June 2026 release. The download page dedicates these downloads under the Open Data Commons **PDDL**. The metro build reads every model tile intersecting the coverage (96 archives; 68 contribute models) and keeps the 76,807 `Current`, `Complete`, and `Approved Demo` models whose centroids fall inside coverage. The exact source URL pattern is `https://maps.bostonplans.org/3d/Bos3d_BldgModels_20260624_OBJ/BOS_G_4_BldgModels_OBJ.zip` (substitute the tile ID).
- **Cambridge buildings:** [Cambridge GIS 3D data](https://www.cambridgema.gov/GIS/3D/3ddata), the 2023 OBJ model tiles C-1 through H-3 (16 tiles, 16,889 models) downloaded from the city's `camb3d` storage. City of Cambridge open data; the catalog's ground elevations are feet, interpreted as such, and no textures are used.
- **Somerville, Brookline, and Chelsea buildings, and Cambridge gaps:** [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) building footprints, © OpenStreetMap contributors, **ODbL 1.0**, retrieved October 28–29, 2026 through the Overpass API for the four town polygons. 28,517 footprints are extruded: 1,587 by `building:levels`, 2 by a `height` tag, and 26,928 by a documented per-type storey default (houses 2.5 floors, apartments 4, offices 4, garages and sheds 1, and so on); house types receive gabled roofs. These are footprint extrusions, not surveyed massing. In Cambridge they fill only blocks the city survey omits.
- **Street centerlines:** [Boston Infrastructure/OpenData layer 8](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/8) for the 15,002 Boston paths. Original geometry, names, and one-way direction flags are retained; vertices closer than half a metre to their neighbour are dropped and a polyline that doubles back on itself is cut, which removes 30 degenerate stubs across both sources (mostly fragments clipped at the town line whose two ends snap to the same Boston endpoint). [Boston's street-segments metadata](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=8f31b239f40847e29543322093c717fc) declares PDDL. The four towns use 5,683 OpenStreetMap road paths from `trunk` to `living_street` (service, pedestrian, and general motorway classes omitted) with widths by class; the six source-aligned Tobin road sections form a specific US 1 exception; 113 way ends are snapped onto Boston centerline ends at the city line so the networks connect.
- **Curbs:** [Boston Infrastructure/OpenData layer 6](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/6) inside Boston; the towns get synthetic curb paths offset from their street centerlines with 3 m filleted corners (the boundary of the union of their pavements, closed by a 3 m disc), a game construction rather than mapped curbs. The survey still traces roadways that have since been removed, so a Boston curb stretch of 8 m or more that lies in parkland (parks grown 3 m) beyond the measuring reach of every street (half the nominal width plus 12 m), OpenStreetMap service road (8 m) and surface lot (4 m) is dropped: 77 stretches, 6.2 km, among them the island between Brookline Avenue and Park Drive and the corridor south of it that the Muddy River restoration turned back into parkland, the Central Artery ramp curbs at North End Park and the Casey Overpass approaches on the Arborway. The Esplanade curbs of Storrow Drive, which the road network does not carry, go with them. The stretches are found on the raw survey (`layer-6.json`, clipped per tile as `build-metro.py` installs it) rather than on the installed curbs, so a rerun finds the same 77 and the count is part of `provenance.streetSurfaces`; the inventory sidewalk parts beyond the same reach, in the same parkland and mostly within 5 m of such a stretch (13 parts: the strips along the removed corridor at Brookline Avenue, the Arborway approaches) are cut with them. Boston Segments also draws a few one-way carriageways through the parkland beside them: Riverway northbound at Longwood runs 1.5 m inside the splitter island, several Arborway segments inside the median, so the nearest curb on one side is the park's own edge and the measurement on the other side crossed the park to the far carriageway (17 m of asphalt over the Riverway island). A station inside a park polygon whose curb within 3 m lies on that park's boundary (within 1 m) runs inside a curbed park edge; before measuring, `build-street-surfaces.py` moves such a centerline onto the roadway, 2.5 m beyond the park's curb (the middle of a lane along it) or to the middle of a narrower roadway ending at the next curb, the move tapering over 12 m into the unmoved stretches with the road's endpoints fixed so junctions keep their connectivity, and writes the moved points back to `city.json` so traffic, lane markings and the asphalt all follow, keeping the source line as `sourcePoints`, which every run restores first so a rerun redoes the same move from the same input (10 roads, 364 stations: Riverway northbound, five Arborway segments, two Claremont Street segments, and stretches of Carleton Street and Neponset Avenue; Ruth Street in Dorchester stays, since its move would cross a building; a move is kept only where it leaves fewer such stations and crosses the same building footprints as the source line, since `scripts/building_clearance.py` generated the road-overlap metadata for that line; a stretch under 3 m, a park corner touching the street, does not count). A station the move cannot take out of the park edge, on a road that ends inside the island, gets its asphalt between that curb and the next curb beyond it on the same side, or the nominal width past the curb when no curb bounds a roadway of at least 2.3 m within the measuring reach (73 stations; both edges then lie on the roadway side of the centerline, the cleaning step below keeps them whatever their neighbours measure, and they do not count towards the measured width); a narrow street a park polygon merely covers, such as Carleton Street along the Southwest Corridor, has its curbs well inside the park and stays measured.
- **Sidewalks:** [Boston Infrastructure/OpenData layer 0, Sidewalk Inventory](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/0) polygons inside Boston with their `MATERIAL` (brick `BR` is drawn as brick, everything else as concrete); the towns have no open sidewalk layer and get a plain 2.9 m band outside each curb.
- **Driveways and parking:** OpenStreetMap `highway=service` ways (20,983 surface ones: driveways, parking aisles, alleys, drive-throughs) and closed `amenity=parking` ways (3,635 surface lots; multi-storey, underground, rooftop and covered parking, tunnels and negative layers are left out) for the whole envelope, fetched by `fetch-metro-sources.py` into `osm-metro-driveways.json`. Neither Boston Segments nor the towns' street classes carry these, but the Boston curb survey encloses many of them, so they are drawn as asphalt rather than as roads (no traffic, routing or grading).
- **Signal locations:** [Boston Infrastructure/OpenData layer 12](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/12) inside Boston, and OpenStreetMap `highway=traffic_signals` nodes in the towns.
- **Terrain:** [USGS 3DEP Bare Earth DEM ImageServer](https://elevation.nationalmap.gov/arcgis/rest/services/3DEPElevation/ImageServer), exported October 28, 2026 for the whole envelope. The source supplies numeric elevation rather than a hillshade picture. [USGS 3DEP products](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program) are available without use restrictions. The raw float32 TIFF, full export request, grid parameters, vertical reference, and source hash are recorded by the preprocessing scripts; provenance is bundled with the game.
- **Neighborhood and town polygons:** [Boston basemap layer 16](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/16) and [layer 17](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/17), using the `name` field and complete official polygon rings.
- **Parks:** [Boston basemap layer 14](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/14), using `SITE_NAME` and `TypeLong`, which also covers the four surrounding towns, supplemented by OpenStreetMap park, garden, and cemetery polygons there. Park geometry is clipped to coverage; existing Copley Square art remains separately authored.
- **Water:** [Boston basemap Hydro polygons layer 12](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Basemaps/Boston_Basemap_Cached_WM/MapServer/12), supplemented by OpenStreetMap water polygons. Overlapping outlines are geometrically united before packaging to avoid drawing two competing water surfaces. The Charles River basin, the Mystic, the Neponset, and the harbor are clipped to the terrain envelope, so water remains visible immediately beyond the developed area.
- **Park paths and additional water detail:** [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), © OpenStreetMap contributors, **ODbL 1.0**. Extracts were retrieved October 27–29, 2026 from the official OSM API and the Overpass API for every mapped park. The raw source, actual request boxes, and method are cached. Footway/path/pedestrian/step centerlines are clipped to official park polygons; tagged bridge segments retain that attribute. Path widths are source tags where present and otherwise documented game assumptions. No OSM map tiles or photographs are bundled. The derived open geographic data and source attribution remain available in the static package and preprocessing scripts.

- **Road inventory inspected, not used as current authoritative lane data:** [layer 14](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/14). Some fields are blank, zero, or carry old dates. The prototype therefore uses documented width assumptions instead of presenting them as current lane measurements.

Street GIS requests use the selected envelope, `inSR=4326`, `outSR=4326`, `outFields=*`, and `spatialRel=esriSpatialRelIntersects`. Object IDs are fetched first and features downloaded in batches to avoid silent record-limit truncation. Raw data is cached for preprocessing rather than requested by each player. Attribution is retained in the application's credits and in `public/data/city.json`.

## Deliberate limitations

- The June 2026 publication date is **not** a fresh survey of every building. Most selected building shapes originate in 2011 observations. Per-model source survey dates, methods, status, and tile IDs are retained in `public/data/provenance.json`.
- A mesh can span several attached addresses or represent one building part. It is not necessarily one parcel or one address.
- `Current`, `Complete`, and `Approved Demo` buildings are included. An approved demolition is still standing according to the catalog. Historical, merely approved, and under-construction models are omitted. Low ancillary walls are omitted. This avoids drawing historical/proposed structures over existing buildings.
- Two survey meshes are structures rather than buildings: the Zakim Bridge model `BOS-2E9SM-83L3W-UVT08`, which includes the Charlestown I-93 viaduct, is kept for its skyline and driven under with exact triangle collision, no foundation skirt, no far-distance box, and a token 6 m footprint, so its 2 km convex hull never blocks streets, sidewalks, the helicopter, or the TD Garden arrival. The Logan terminal complex `BOS-4WO5X-6OJI5-G5S26` keeps its footprint but also uses exact collision, since its hull would close the terminal road loop. Two more survey complexes keep their footprints but collide as exact meshes for the same reason: the BUMC ambulatory care center `BOS-HO11N-VJBYE-3RDXF`, whose convex hull overlaps about 216 m of Massachusetts Avenue, and NEXUS at the Allston Innovation Corridor `BOS-3R76C-CGBE8-V3V1H`, an L whose hull would close about 176 m of Western Avenue that no source triangle covers.
- Downloaded OBJ exports contain no photographic façade textures. Runtime façades are original procedural materials. No Google, Nearmap, or Mapillary photographs are bundled.
- Façade selection does not analyze satellite imagery. Reviewed survey-ID identities and religious building/amenity tags take precedence over name cues, followed by height/footprint heuristics. `src/data/building-identities.json` supplies the same identity and name rules to the renderer and Python compiler. Numeric Church/Temple Street addresses and explicitly named housing are not religious-use evidence. Missing semantic metadata can still require a reviewed identity correction.
- **Mission Church correction:** Boston's F6 catalog has two `Current` meshes for the Basilica of Our Lady of Perpetual Help: `BOS-ADQVO-277LT-NQB6N` (`1545 TREMONT ST`, 2021 survey) and `BOS-U59XZ-OVMVF-D1IT5` (`1545 -1565 TREMONT ST`, 2011 survey), both on parcel `1000012000`, with blank `StructUse`. The old church/chapel/cathedral name check missed these address-only names; their 70.79/71.57 m spire heights triggered the >65 m office/glass fallback. Both IDs now have an explicit church identity (original names retained as `sourceName`), consistent distant masonry, and a dedicated stone/round-arch/rose-window material treatment guided by the user-provided aerial and street photographs. Material partitions retain all surveyed positions and normals, including the overlapping catalog shells; the photos are references, not bundled texture assets.
- Storefront glazing, signs, and awnings are original interpretive art. Their labels are fictional, and their street-facing placement is checked against the real building mesh; they do not reproduce actual businesses.
- Roof-mounted air-conditioning units and fans are original interpretive props. Their full clearance area is checked against flat source roof triangles; they do not claim to reproduce actual rooftop installations.
- Projecting window trim, cornices, stoops, and railings are original game geometry attached to compatible source surfaces. The window grid follows the procedural material coordinates; these details are not individual building surveys.
- Copley Square landscaping interprets the [current Boston Parks illustrative site plan](https://www.boston.gov/departments/parks-and-recreation/project/copley-square-park-improvements), reviewed October 26, 2026. Raised grove, fountain-side groves, plaza, small Trinity-side lawn, and seating are approximate original meshes aligned to the surrounding road grid. No plan images are bundled, and this is not a measured as-built landscape model.
- Player/traffic models, pedestrian figures, foliage, and props are original procedural art. Parking placement and pedestrian routes are checked for road/building clearance; they do not claim current legal parking rules or measured footfall.
- Sound effects were generated with ElevenLabs, not captured in Boston. Prompts, durations, model ID, and asset hashes are retained in `public/audio/provenance.json`; the API key is used only by the local generation script.
- The title screen's background, `public/title-scene.jpg`, is an in-game screenshot of this prototype, not a photograph. Its Bebas Neue, Barlow, and JetBrains Mono typefaces are bundled from the `@fontsource` npm packages under the SIL Open Font License 1.1; no font service is contacted at runtime.
- Hills come from the **actual bare-earth DEM**, packaged at 6-meter spacing. Bilinear export resampling and a gentle 3×3 `[1,2,1]` separable smoothing pass suppress small retaining-wall/kerb aliasing; these are not centimeter-accurate road surfaces or constructed ramps. The terrain ranges from about 80 meters below the Back Bay starting datum (the harbor floor) to about 111 meters above it in Brighton and Brookline. The Parker Hill Avenue destination is approximately 58.82 meters above the datum.
- Street levelling (`scripts/grade-terrain.mjs`) cannot build a retaining wall. Where the source centerlines leave two streets at grade within a few metres of each other while the DEM puts one of them several metres lower (the South Boston Haul Road in its cut beside the Dorchester Avenue bridge abutments), the pavements meet through a short bank instead of a wall: the Haul Road keeps its underpass beneath the Dorchester Avenue deck, and no bank left between separated pavements exceeds about 7% (the steepest is now Massachusetts Avenue at the Memorial Drive ramps; the Haul Road abutment cell was 39% before the landings were held inside the solver). Approach embankments that the sources carry no level for (the Memorial Drive ramps, Mountfort Street and Saint Mary's Street beside the Turnpike cut) keep a bank across part of their assumed pavement width where the DEM crest is narrower than the street; the BU Bridge approach and First Street in Cambridge are below 6%, and Frontage Road on the Expressway fill keeps one 8% section at its junction. Across the metro, 27 pavement sections on 11 roads still bank beyond 6%, all at junctions or beside the Turnpike cut. `tests/road-grade-coverage.test.mjs` counts the grade separations, holds the banked sections to a short list with a drivable ceiling, and audits the rest of the network against the original crossfall limits.
- Street levelling grades the whole **street corridor**: the pavement and a 2.9 m sidewalk allowance on each side (`WALK` in `src/simulation/road-grading.ts`, the width of the rendered sidewalk ribbon) are held level across their width by crossfall constraints on every grid triangle the corridor crosses, and the blend back to the natural ground starts beyond the sidewalk rather than under it. On a side-hill street the corridor datum drops by the cross fall to the lower edge (at most 1.5 m below the centerline sample), so the street is cut into the slope: downhill houses keep their exposed fronts and uphill houses stand on foundation walls, which the runtime draws as skirts. The drop applies only where the cross-section is a slope; a street along a terrace edge, a cut or an embankment (Washington Place above the South Boston Haul Road) keeps its centerline datum. Footprint edges inside the corridor are not protected from the grading; outside it, fill stays capped at about 0.25 m above a building's base and cuts at small houses at 0.5 m, the same modest allowance as before, because footprints held near their natural ground are also what keeps a street beside a cut from being dragged down into it by the crossfall solver. Two guards keep the corridor solver from spreading a conflict it cannot resolve: a footprint protection may not cut a cell more than a metre below its natural ground (a protected sample near the edge of a cell shared with a levelled sidewalk would otherwise lever the cell's far corner metres down, the trench that opened beside 125 Centre Street), and the sidewalk part of a corridor leaves out any cell that also carries the roadbed of a street more than 2.5 m higher or lower (Fort Avenue on its terrace above Centre Street, the streets above the South Boston Haul Road cut), so the bank between them stays steep instead of one street sliding toward the other; the pavement itself is always levelled, because a cell skipped under a travelled lane leaves a wall inside the lane (West Side Drive above its service road), which is worse than a terrace settling toward the lower street. The final solver stage releases crossfall to 2 percent, where the earlier grader allowed 4.5. Summer Street in Somerville, on the north slope of Spring Hill, is the fixture for this (`tests/road-grade-coverage.test.mjs`): before the corridor model its sidewalks followed the 10 to 14 percent cross slope of the 6 m grid and the pavement tilted a different way at every cell.
- Buildings retain source horizontal placement and relative massing. An audit found that the catalog's historical `Gnd_El_Ft` field contains metre-scale ground estimates, while OBJ coordinates are feet. New-area primary models use metre-correct ground normalization, with their actual source floor seated at the highest DEM sample around the footprint minus 0.1 meter. Runtime foundation skirts close the downhill gap. This is a practical terrain seating approximation, not an as-built foundation survey; large sloping sites may have visible retaining bases.
- **Supported upper parts remain elevated:** source components that sit on another model share that support model's translation, rather than being individually dropped to the street. Their `partOf` relationship requires at least 90% footprint support and compatible original vertical extents. The validator independently checks both. Low ancillary meshes are omitted. Three additional central-city source files contain datum/helper vertices or collapsed underground triangles; normalization uses the lowest surviving visible face to prevent these helpers from leaving the building suspended above its base.
- The original 164 Back Bay models retain their legacy vertical normalization so the already authored landmark wall planes and silhouettes remain unchanged. This compatibility policy, the catalogue value, selected normalization ground in meters, original source min/max elevations, and any ground-contact offset are recorded per model in `public/data/provenance.json`.
- **Selected Bowker/Charlesgate structures are now independent upper roadways:** 30 retained City GIS centerline segments restore the Bowker carriageways/ramps and connecting Boylston bridge approaches, with two additional existing Commonwealth Avenue paths replaced by lower bridge decks across the Muddy River, without changing map coverage. Source `F_ZLEV`/`T_ZLEV` topology and endpoint elevations keep Commonwealth Avenue, Beacon Street, and Ipswich Street below the overpass. [MassDOT’s Bowker project](https://www.mass.gov/bowker-overpass-project) confirms the real crossing. These are the retained GIS alignment, not a reconstruction of current construction staging or the future replacement design. The 6.4-meter local-datum main deck and 0.75-meter slab thickness are explicit game estimates, with smooth ramps meeting the existing terrain; they are not measured bridge engineering. Rendered top/underside geometry, Rapier collision, and road-aware traffic heights share the same profile. Supports avoid mapped lower streets. Elsewhere in the region, 527 road segments whose source level topology or water crossing marks them as bridges receive the same generic 6.4 m deck profile with smooth approach ramps, which connects Cambridge, Charlestown, and the Seaport across the Charles, the Mystic, and Fort Point Channel; these are estimated profiles on the real alignments, not surveyed decks. Eighteen of those segments restore source-path gaps cut out by district clipping across water, including Harvard, both Longfellow carriageways, Seaport Boulevard, Congress Street, Summer Street, Alford Street, and North Washington Street. `public/data/bridge-spans-source.json` records those original Boston GIS curves; `scripts/restore-water-crossings.py` resamples them every 3 m, interpolates between the exact retained endpoint heights, and unions only narrow traffic corridors into the runtime coverage. Existing graded shore landings remain intact. Longfellow’s single carriageways use 7 m widths rather than treating the OSM whole-bridge width as one lane. Interstate/freeway classes, expressways/turnpikes, Storrow Drive and other tunnels remain outside the surface street network (34 Boston centerline IDs are omitted by that base-build filter); the separate Central Artery sidecar restores selected underground alignments, while the new Chelsea Creek bridges provide a road route to East Boston.
- Roads use authentic centerline paths. Surface widths are prototype assumptions in meters: Boylston 13, Newbury 10, Commonwealth carriageways 9, Marlborough 9, Beacon 12, Dartmouth 11, Clarendon 11, Berkeley 12, Exeter 10, Huntington 15, Saint James 12, Stuart 13, Arlington 14, Columbus 12, Tremont 12, Parker 9, Parker Hill 8, Francis/Longwood 12, Calumet/Fisher/Hillside 8, Saint Alphonsus 9; other Boston streets by their functional class (arterials 14 and 12, collectors 10, local streets 7); alleys 4.2; OpenStreetMap trunk 14, primary 13, secondary 11, tertiary 9, residential 7, living streets 6. Source one-way flags are retained, but traffic behavior and striping are simplified.
- Boylston lane configuration and Copley Square landscaping have changed since the source surveys. The scene is a driving prototype, not a current transportation or navigation product.

## Neighborhood architecture and Fenway Park

Mission Hill's triple-decker treatment is an **inferred architectural profile**, not an address-level inventory. The [Boston Planning Mission Hill overview](https://www.bostonplans.org/neighborhoods/mission-hill/at-a-glance) and [City of Boston Mission Hill Triangle description](https://content.boston.gov/historic-district/mission-hill-triangle-architectural-conservation-district) provide neighborhood context. Runtime selection in `src/render/neighborhood-profiles.ts` uses the official polygons of the wood-frame districts (Mission Hill, Jamaica Plain, Roxbury, Dorchester, Mattapan, South Boston, Charlestown, East Boston, Allston, Brighton, Cambridge, Somerville, Brookline and Chelsea), measured source height of 8.3–14.4 m, footprint area of 65–245 m², separation from adjoining buildings, and frontage toward residential streets. Churches, schools, hospitals, and named landmarks are excluded. In the current package, 206 Mission Hill source masses qualify for the full porch treatment. A house whose street front cannot be verified (its street is a shopping corridor, or no wall of 5.5–15 m faces a street) still receives the clapboard family without porches. In the towns, a street's residential or commercial role comes from its OpenStreetMap class (`kind` on the road) and a short list of shopping corridors rather than from its width or from the Boston street-name lists: OpenStreetMap widths are class defaults or tagged carriageway widths (Somerville's Summer Street is tagged 13.7 m), and downtown names recur in the towns. OpenStreetMap records a building material for only 12 Somerville, 4 Brookline and 162 Cambridge buildings and tags 79 percent of Somerville's buildings as plain `building=yes`, so the source does not say brick or wood; the building tag (`kind` on the building) only excludes retail, office, garage, institutional and similar kinds. The far-city boxes (`facade_style` in `scripts/build-metro.py`) apply the same size and district rule so near and far colors agree. This is a conservative game-art heuristic; those addresses have not been individually verified as wood-framed three-family buildings.

The selected houses keep their source positions, footprints, bay projections, roofs, and relative massing. Five original clapboard palettes, three-floor window spacing, projecting trim, cornices, and stacked porches convey the local housing type. Window/porch placement is checked against source wall geometry and available street setback; this validates fit, not historical architectural accuracy. Generic masonry storefront details and roof clutter are suppressed for these candidates. Stair runs solve against terrain at their actual lower landing, with ground-reaching masonry and porch footings. The 0.65 m of wall below the raised ground floor is rendered as exposed foundation (footing concrete with a basement light under each window bay) by the facade shader, because the periodic window texture would otherwise show the head of a window row there, half buried above the footing skirt. Tight front setbacks use side entries with matching railing openings; sites without room for a plausible flight omit the added procedural porch. Decorative steps and rails are not separately surveyed or fully collision-modeled.

Fenway's main shell is Boston model `BOS-73AP7-T4BL0-O4QDM` (catalog name `24 JERSEY ST`), with source stadium components including its 2003 expansion. It already has an open field; no generic roof is substituted. `src/data/fenway-detail.json` adds an **ODbL-derived OpenStreetMap extract**, © OpenStreetMap contributors, obtained from the [official OSM map API](https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/map?bbox=-71.1002,42.3452,-71.0945,42.3484). The stored features are ways 813683133 (field), 813683131 (inner infield), 1339130771 (Green Monster), 813683146 (Monster seats), 148755799 and 148755801 (bullpens), 813683134 (bleachers), and 813683142, 813683143, 813683144, and 814360194 (stands). Mapped markers locate the right-field foul pole and center-field scoreboard. Source IDs, request URL, and interpretive limitations are bundled with the derived coordinates; [OSM attribution and license](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) apply.

The [Red Sox official Fenway facts and figures](https://www.mlb.com/redsox/ballpark/facts-figures) provide the short 310-foot left-field and 302-foot right-field dimensions and Green Monster height. The field layout solves home plate against the mapped foul-pole positions, then constructs a 90-foot basepath square and 60-foot-6-inch pitching distance. Original warning-track, clay, grass, seat, wall, scoreboard, light-bank, and sign geometry completes the landmark. Seat height follows the highest supporting surface across all four source stadium components, including the separately modeled lower bowl; shallow concrete row treads distinguish seating from retained roof surfaces. Street-facing gate/window bays are fitted to measured wall planes. The decorative seating arrangement, red seat, lettering, and scoreboard artwork are interpretations, not a current seat-by-seat survey or live baseball information. No MLB photographs, textures, or third-party stadium model are bundled. Fenway’s main shell uses its original source triangles for collision, preserving the open field. The generated field, seats, and stand details remain decorative and are not a fully playable or collision-modeled stadium interior.


The complex buildings using exact mesh collision (Fenway Park, the Prudential complex, 145 Dartmouth, the BUMC ambulatory care center, NEXUS, and the Logan terminals) also use source-edge foundations; the Zakim Bridge structure uses exact collision without one. The visible strips and static collision share identical triangles. No convex skirt or horizontal cap is added, so real street openings, the stadium field, and higher overhangs remain open. ## Original environment and ambient characters

Tree species, individual tree placement, pedestrians, and ground-cover details are **original procedural art**, not a municipal vegetation or population inventory. Maple, elm, honey-locust, and white-pine silhouettes use generated leaf masks and branching geometry. Street planting uses broadleaf variants; park planting may include pine. The same branch/leaf resources are shared across instances, with three distance levels and a bounded nearby active field. Wind deformation also applies to the foliage shadow pass. No downloaded leaf photographs or tree models are used.

Park grass combines generated blade/color and relief textures with broad spatial color variation and a capped set of nearby geometric tufts. Tufts are constrained to selected mapped park polygons and excluded from mapped paths, road margins, building footprints, and water. These procedural lawn treatments do not claim actual mowing patterns or plant coverage. Small reflective puddles are placed along suitable near-level road edges; their outlines and positions are fictional, they use the scene's environment reflections, and there is no rain, drainage, or planar-reflection simulation.

River and pond ripples are original procedural art. `src/render/water-material.ts` generates one shared 256 × 256 normal texture from a periodic wave spectrum, then drifts it at three scales in world coordinates so tiles share the same pattern and time. Filtered normals, water's dielectric index of refraction, and the existing sky/local environment probe provide sun highlights and Fresnel reflections without displacing the horizontal surface or adding a reflection render pass. Ripple strength and drift speed use the complete body area and characteristic width, so the sheltered Fens stays nearly still while the broad Charles keeps wind ripples. Named river reaches share settings across source and tile seams. Mobile uses the sky environment. These are environment-map reflections, not planar mirrors or a fluid simulation.

Pedestrians use original articulated instanced meshes with sculpted faces, blinking eyes, varied hairlines, tapered clothing, rounded shoes, and varied skin/hair/clothing colors, adult proportions, and backpacks. Face color detail, hair strands, and fabric weave are generated locally as shared textures; no photographs or external character assets are used. Faces use simpler geometry beyond 65 meters. Their distance-driven walk cycle keeps the stance foot planted with heel/toe roll, opposite arm swing, body sway, and blended stops. A bounded pool of up to 112 actors walks clearance-checked sidewalk stretches, targeting 72 nearby walkers in busy commercial districts, 44 in mixed districts, and 26 in residential districts, and shifts distant actors toward the active neighborhood; each route remains grounded by the same terrain query as the scene. They pause near a moving player car, but do not simulate real footfall, road crossing, destinations, or a full crowd-navigation system. The prototype remains free roaming: there are no delivery, checkpoint, or scoring missions.

## Coordinates and conversion

The city models use Boston-shifted Massachusetts State Plane feet (NAD83 / EPSG:2249). Full State Plane coordinates are local model coordinates plus X=731,100 feet, Y=2,902,900 feet. The game adds a second origin shift to keep floating-point coordinates small:

```
world X = (model east feet − 40,000) × (1200 / 3937)
world Z = −(model north feet − 50,000) × (1200 / 3937)
source elevation meters = model elevation feet × (1200 / 3937)
world Y = max(0, source elevation meters − normalizationGroundMeters)
          + building.baseElevation
```

Positive X points east, negative Z points north, and positive Y points up. GIS coordinates are transformed to EPSG:2249 before these offsets are applied. Road geometry is clipped to curated coverage; building meshes with centroids inside it may extend a little beyond the outline. New-area normalization uses the greater of the metre-scale catalog ground and the primary model's minimum source elevation; supported upper components inherit that primary model's normalization and placement. The original Back Bay compatibility branch preserves its prior feet interpretation. `height` remains the model's highest point above its shared base. **GLB Y already includes `baseElevation`; the runtime must not translate it a second time.**

The terrain grid is `{width:2774,height:3039,cellSize:6,originX:-8946,originZ:-8046}` with its samples in the gzip-compressed int16 centimetre sidecar `elevation.bin` (`city.elevation.file`, `scale: 0.01`, `encoding: "int16-gzip"`). Samples are row-major, with X increasing across a row and world Z increasing down the rows. Heights are meters relative to the DEM elevation at the original Boylston spawn: **3.385349780189643 meters** at world `(-291.674,36.660)`. The package range is about −80.2 to +110.8 meters. This local zero is not a claim that all Back Bay streets are level. Rendering and physics use the same NE–SW cell diagonal and piecewise-linear height query. `src/simulation/road-grading.ts` derives the drivable grid from the DEM: street centerline profiles are smoothed, connected intersections share heights, and paved road widths are leveled with short shoulders blending into the adjoining terrain. The ground-level end of every bridge deck is a hard datum: the terrain triangle under each landing is held at the deck's surveyed height inside every constraint pass (the landing point alone, because neighbouring sub-10 m deck pieces land at different heights and pinning whole lines lets one small cell absorb the conflict), so approach streets climb to the abutment instead of being levelled into the lower road and forced back up at the end. Source elevations at building centers and footprint edges bound road fill and may lower pavement vertices, but never more than 3 m below the source ground; foundation skirts connect such modest terrain cuts beneath the fixed models. For the metro package that correction is run offline by `scripts/grade-terrain.mjs` (the same code, on 90 overlapping 2 km windows in worker threads); `elevation.bin` ships the graded grid, `elevation-source.bin` the untouched DEM export, and `city.elevation.grading` records the road count, changed cells, maximum cut and fill, and the window layout (chunk count and the chunk, margin and band sizes); wall-clock time and the worker count are not part of the package, because the grid does not depend on them. Holding the landings inside the solver took the Dorchester Avenue abutment beside the South Boston Haul Road from a 22% crossfall and a 39% internal bank to 3.6% and 7.9%, and cut the number of 2 m road sections banked 6% or more across their full width from 194 to 17 metro-wide. This removes bare-earth bank and depression artifacts from the driving surface while retaining broad uphill/downhill changes. The building meshes are not rewritten. This is procedural roadbed construction, not a surveyed road-elevation dataset; bridge decks remain separate levels and meet the graded ground within a centimetre at their landings.

## Rebuild

Python 3.11+ with the pinned `pyproj`, NumPy, and Shapely dependencies and Node.js 24 are sufficient. The package is a city index plus binary sidecars:

- `city.json`: origin, bounds, coverage outlines, spawn, sources, the elevation and tile manifests, every road, signal, park, water body, neighborhood, and destination, and `landmarkBuildings`, the buildings the landmark modules address before their tiles load. It holds no building list of its own.
- `tiles/t_{tx}_{tz}.bin` (DBT2, 202 files): one gzip tile per 1 km square containing the buildings whose centers fall in it (metadata plus their quantized shells: int16 positions at 1 cm around a per-building anchor, or a coarser `scale` of 2, 5, or 10 cm when a very large mesh would overflow int16; the Prudential and the Logan terminal complex use 2 cm and the Zakim Bridge 5 cm), the curbs, park paths, and road IDs in the square, and the street surfaces described below (`pavements`, `borderPavements`, `sidewalks`, `islands`, `driveways`). `scripts/tileformat.py` writes and `src/simulation/tile-format.ts` reads the same layout; facade and roof triangles are split by normal and separate vertices are deduplicated.
- `far.bin` (DBF2): every building's convex footprint at decimetre precision with height, base, facade family, and flags (landmark, exact-mesh, hidden, massing fragment), for the far-distance boxes, the helicopter's obstacle index, sidewalk clearance, and building lookup before a tile loads. Sprawling complexes over 60 m tall with more than 15,000 m² of hull, or any hull over 100,000 m², are hidden and followed by terraced fragments (connected 20 m cells above 10, 25, 50, and 75% of the height) so a podium with towers does not draw as one slab.
- `elevation.bin` and `elevation-source.bin`: the graded and raw DEM grids described above.
- `provenance.json`: sources, per-model normalization policies, tile and grading parameters, omitted road IDs, and the Bowker structure assumptions.

At load time, wall UV projection is stabilized across nearby triangle orientations, with window proportions defined by the assigned procedural architectural family; source horizontal geometry and relative massing are retained.

Survey-shell orientation is checked when packaging both DBT2 tiles and the legacy GLB. A consistently wound component with negative enclosed volume and inward-facing upper roof surfaces is reversed as a component, preserving its measured vertices and triangle membership. Open-floor shells require at least 70% of their open boundary length at the floor; nested cavity components, inconsistent/nonmanifold components, and surveyed bridge structures are not inferred as occupied solids. Correctly oriented roofs, soffits, floors, and courtyards retain their winding. `scripts/audit-building-shells.py` audits every packaged source and accepts `--repair` for existing packages; the validator rejects the same clear inversions. The Mattapan River Street report identified Boston survey mesh `BOS-NA8BR-YJOW2-4D067`, not an OpenStreetMap extrusion. The subsequent metro audit repaired 64 components (1,481 triangles) in 20 surveyed buildings across 19 tiles: 19 Boston models and one Cambridge model. All 23,902 OpenStreetMap extrusions passed this orientation check. No measured positions, roof shapes, footprints, terrain, or source metadata were regenerated.

Decorative masonry stoops now share the terrain-aware stair solver used by wood porches. The upper platform, treads and toe pad extend below the terrain across their footprints, flight length/riser count follows the complete ground descent, and railings follow the resulting flight. Side flights are permitted where frontage clearance supports them; a decorative entrance is omitted if no flight fits the setback without intersecting a neighbour. Neighbour clearance matches the owner's decimetre DBF2 hull by footprint and envelope, since far hulls have no building IDs. This avoids treating a building's own coarse hull as a neighbouring obstruction. Run `.venv-data/bin/python scripts/test_mesh_winding.py`, `node --test tests/mattapan-buildings.test.mjs tests/porch-stairs.test.mjs`, and `npm test -- tests/mattapan-buildings.spec.ts` for the shell/stoop regressions.


Generated OpenStreetMap buildings also carry `eaveHeight`, the extrusion's wall height above its base, separately from total ridge height. Wood-frame floor spacing uses that wall height; two-storey walls do not get three-storey porches. Batched façades carry their own window-height limit: a window row whose full opening and lintel cannot fit below the eaves uses the window-free wall finish instead, with no interior, night glow or projecting window trim. Gable caps use outward horizontal normals; testing these vertical triangles against an upward vector had allowed one end to face inward and disappear through backface culling. The October 28 repair checked all 23,902 extrusions and reversed 13,343 inward gable ends across 51 updated tiles, preserving every quantized vertex and the surveyed meshes. `scripts/repair-osm-roofs.py` applies this idempotent migration to older packages using their recorded normalization eaves. New builds emit the metadata and correct winding directly; the data validator rejects missing eaves or inward gable caps. The shipped town tiles have since been rebuilt from source rather than migrated (see the reproducibility note below), so their wall quads are split along the oriented ring direction the generator has used since this fix, with the same vertices and outward normals as the migrated tiles. Run `.venv-data/bin/python scripts/test_osm_buildings.py` for rotated/reversed-footprint and courtyard generator regressions, and `npm test -- tests/building-roofs.spec.ts` for the reported Lowell/Summer Street views in golden-hour and night lighting.

**Reproducibility.** The chain is a pure function of the source cache, the pinned baseline, the checked-in sidecars and the scripts: two runs from the same `data-source-cache/` write byte-identical `tiles/*.bin`, `far.bin`, `elevation.bin`, `elevation-source.bin`, `city.json` and `provenance.json`, so a rebuild after a script or source change diffs only what the change touched, and a rebuild into a staging directory verifies the shipped package. Three things were fixed on 28 October 2026 to get there. The Python writers (`tileformat.write_gzip`) emit gzip streams with a zero timestamp, no file name and the unknown-OS byte, the same header shape Node's zlib writes for the graded grid, where `gzip.open` had stamped every tile and far file with the build time. `grade-terrain.mjs` blends its 90 solver windows in chunk order after the worker pool drains instead of in arrival order: float addition is not associative, so the old order moved cells sitting on a centimetre boundary from one run to the next while the recorded statistics stayed the same (the previously shipped grid differs from the chunk-ordered result in one of its 8.4 million cells, by 1 cm, with the same recorded statistics), and the grid now depends on `--workers` only for wall-clock time (runs with 10 and 6 workers are byte-identical). The town tiles had been migrated in place by `repair-osm-roofs.py` rather than rebuilt and still carried the pre-fix ring direction of their wall quads; they were regenerated once. Python's hash randomization does not reach the output (builds under different `PYTHONHASHSEED` values are identical: the compiler iterates no string-keyed set unsorted). Byte identity is a guarantee for one toolchain (the pinned Python packages' GEOS and zlib, and the Node release's zlib); another zlib may compress identical content into a different stream and another GEOS may buffer or triangulate differently, so packages built on different machines are compared by content. `scripts/compare-metro.py A B` reports, file by file, byte identity, identical content behind a different gzip header, or which tile buildings, header sections and manifest keys differ, and `validate-city.py` rejects any shipped gzip stream that carries a timestamp or file name. `packagingDate` in `provenance.json` is a constant in `build-metro.py`, not the build time.

Validation parses every tile and the far file and checks the manifest against the files shipped, unique IDs filed in the tile of their center, coverage, normalization policies, finite quantized positions, index ranges, documented base and height per building, at least 90% footprint support for upper parts, ground contact for every nonlegacy primary model, the structure and exact-mesh sets, the retained Back Bay churches and models, the graded and raw grids with the recorded grading statistics and the spawn datum, road widths, one-way flags, coverage, and deck profiles, the absence of limited-access highway names, spawn clearance, all **56** destinations with safe in-roadway arrivals outside buildings, the 26 district names and excluded neighborhoods, the major park outlines, the Mission Hill and Brigham Circle elevations, and continuous undirected height-aware road connections from Back Bay to at least 90% of the network and to every mainland destination. Validation does not certify legal routing or present-day lane rules. The geographic assets occupy approximately **100 MB** before HTTP compression: 67.01 MB of tiles (the street surfaces added about 12.1 MB), 10.94 MB graded and 11.02 MB raw elevation, 3.89 MB far hulls, 7.10 MB city JSON, and 22 KB provenance. The raw elevation grid is read only by tests and tools, so a session fetches about 22 MB plus its nearby tiles.

The application can therefore run from a static host without a geographic-data API key or external map calls.

## Street surfaces between the curbs

Until 30 October 2026 every ground-level road was drawn as two constant-width ribbons from its `width` field: asphalt at the class or name guess (7 m for a local street, 10 m for Fenway, 15 m for Huntington Avenue) and a sidewalk at that width plus 5.8 m. The curbs come from a different source, the Boston survey (or, in the towns, the synthetic offset lines), so wherever the real roadway differs from the guess the two disagreed: the median measured roadway is 1.12 times the guessed width and a quarter of Boston's streets are over 1.4 times it (the Riverway and Brookline Avenue parkways, Forsyth Way with its pull-off bay, Louis Prang Street with an off-centre centerline). That produced every reported curb fault: curbs standing inside the concrete or out on the asphalt, square-cornered sidewalk ribbons crossing the curb returns at corners, bump-outs with asphalt inside the curb, Green Line platforms and lane dividers drawn as hollow curbs over asphalt, and curbs around grass where a curbed paved area has no centerline.

`scripts/build-street-surfaces.py` (library `scripts/street_surfaces.py`, after `build-chelsea-places.py` in the chain) measures the roadway instead. From 1 m stations along every surface road (decks and tunnels keep their ribbons) it casts a ray each way, perpendicular to the centerline (bisected at vertices), as far as half the guessed width plus 12 m, and takes the nearest curb hit. The per-side distance series is cleaned: runs shorter than 7 m that depart more than 1.5 m from the local median (±4 stations) are dropped (driveway tongues outward, a median curb crossed at a junction inward), gaps up to 40 m are interpolated between their neighbours, longer unmeasured stretches ramp to the guessed half width over 6 m, and no edge comes within 1.2 m of the centerline. Station pairs are then thinned to the ones that move either edge more than 3 cm off the chord. Each road's record goes into the tile that owns it (the half-length point, as `road-tile-coverage.ts` decides) as `pavements`: `[lx, lz, rx, rz, ...]` in centimetres from the tile origin plus a `walk` flag per side, and `city.json` gains `pavedWidth` (median left plus median right) on the 16,768 roads measured on both sides at five or more stations. The tile builder drapes the asphalt between the two edges with the same subdivision and normal averaging as a ribbon (`drapedStripInto` in `src/render/terrain-ground.ts`), so the curb now stands at the asphalt edge everywhere a curb was surveyed.

Sidewalks come from the [Boston Sidewalk Inventory, Infrastructure/OpenData layer 0](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Infrastructure/OpenData/MapServer/0) (fetched by `fetch-metro-sources.py` with the other GIS layers): 23,676 records in the study envelope, 27,836 polygon parts after clipping to the package coverage (Esri rings run clockwise for exteriors and counter-clockwise for holes), grown 0.35 m so they tuck under the asphalt edge, clipped into 30,270 tile parts and drawn 3 cm above the ground (above the 2.4 cm lawn decals, below the 4.5 cm asphalt). `MATERIAL` `BR` is drawn as brick; concrete (`CC`), bituminous and the unrecorded ones share the concrete texture. The `walk` flag of a roadway edge is set when at least half of the probes 1 m outside it, every fifth station, land in an inventory polygon; a side without one (the towns, which have no open sidewalk layer, and the Boston streets the inventory misses) keeps a plain 2.9 m band following the curb edge, the `WALK` allowance the terrain grading levels. An inventory polygon that lies mostly in parkland and holds a disc 12 m across, wider than four times its recorded `SWK_WIDTH` (or with that width blank), outlines a whole island or mall rather than its pavement: the island at Brookline Avenue and Park Drive is recorded as a 9.5 ft sidewalk around 2,224 m², the Columbia Road mall as a 12 ft one around 58,943 m². The 19 such polygons keep only their recorded width (2.9 m when blank) along the boundary, so the lawn inside stays lawn. The 13 parts that followed removed roadways are cut as described under curbs.

Closed curb faces (`shapely.polygonize` over the noded curb union, 2,853 faces) that hold no surface centerline and no building centre are classified next. A face that an OpenStreetMap service road enters (reaching at least 0.5 m inside its curb, not merely grazing it) or that a surface parking lot covers by more than half is paved whole, up to 6,000 m² (18 faces: curbed turnarounds and lots the survey enclosed without a road). Of the rest, faces with less than half their area in a park are the candidates for a concrete fill: faces an inventory sidewalk reaches are left to that sidewalk; faces up to 400 m², or thinner than 3.5 m by the 2·area/perimeter proxy, become `islands` (184 of them, drawn 5 cm above the ground): the Huntington Avenue Green Line platforms, lane dividers and traffic islands. Larger bare faces (78) are planted medians and parkway islands and stay terrain; faces over 2,500 m² are blocks.

`driveways` are the asphalt that no street centerline carries. Every surface OSM service road is measured to the surveyed curbs like a street, with rays reaching half its `width` tag (feet converted, clamped to 2.5..12 m) or class width (parking aisles 5.5 m, driveways 4 m, alleys 4.5 m, otherwise 4.5 m) plus 6 m, a shorter reach than the streets' 12 m so a parallel street's curb stays out of it. An OSM service line is not a centerline (at the MFA loop it wanders across the driveway and for 24 m runs just outside the forecourt curb), so the caster keeps the two nearest curbs each way. Where the line touches a curb (under 0.3 m) on one side and finds nothing on the other, the next curb beyond the touched one decides: at about that line's median curb-to-curb width (its class width when fewer than five stations measured both sides) the asphalt lies across the touched curb, between the two; otherwise the line is inside the driveway and the open side is completed to that width. The floor past a curb is 10 cm, under the curb box, instead of the streets' 1.2 m; lines with no curb in reach anywhere keep a ribbon of the class width. The surface lots and the paved curb faces are added; the whole set is dissolved (so no two asphalt faces share a height), clipped to the package coverage and cut around the inventory sidewalks (a driveway apron keeps its concrete), then clipped per tile: 7,353 surfaces in 8,451 tile parts, drawn 3.5 cm above the ground, above the lawn decals and the sidewalks and below the 4.5 cm roadway asphalt, so a service way that duplicates a mapped street hides under it. The loop at the MFA's Fenway entrance is the motivating case: the survey curbed it, the Fenway parkway polygon covers it, and only OSM carries the driveway, so without this layer it read as a curbed lawn. The towns' synthetic curbs are regenerated at the same time: the union of each town's street pavements (half the guessed width plus 9 cm) is closed by a 3 m disc (buffer out, buffer in) so every curb return at an intersection is a fillet instead of a mitre; the mitred chunks `build-metro.py` writes are matched exactly and removed, so the installed total is 44,918 surveyed plus 5,128 synthetic curb paths (the survey count follows each tile's split between its survey curbs and the appended fillets, not a midpoint test). The script is idempotent and deterministic: rerunning it on an installed package rewrites identical bytes and changes no tile, and `validate-city.py` checks that every surface road has a record, that no recorded roadway is narrower than 2.3 m or wider than 60 m, that the measured widths stay between 1.5 and 45 m, and that `provenance.streetSurfaces` matches the installed counts. Street furniture, walkers, parked cars, meters and storefront setbacks use `pavedWidth` where it exists so trees and lamps stand beyond the measured curb rather than the guessed one, and traffic lane offsets scale with it too: a one-way avenue of class width 12 m or more carries two lanes only where the measured roadway is at least 6.5 m wide, so on Huntington Avenue, whose one-way carriageways beside the Green Line reservation measure about 7.5 m against the 15 m class guess and whose Copley Place lane measures 5.3 m, cars stay between the curbs instead of straddling the sidewalk. Not measured: deck and tunnel roads, which keep the ribbon, and curbed paved areas without a centerline that OpenStreetMap does not map as a service road or lot, which stay terrain. Run `PYTHONPATH=scripts .venv-data/bin/python -m unittest scripts/test_street_surfaces.py` for the measurement, cleaning, fillet, island, driveway, stale-curb, park-edge move and placement, border-record and idempotence checks on synthetic input, and `node --test tests/street-surfaces.test.mjs` for the strip builder, the crosswalk spans and the shipped Fenway, Brookline Avenue, Riverway and Edwin H. Land Boulevard windows. Crosswalk stripes span the roadway measured at their own station (`pavementSpan`), widened across the record of a twin carriageway (`roadwaySpan`: edges running the same way there, overlapping or touching the roadway within half a metre, and adding at most one roadway of the crosswalk's own width plus 2 m beyond it), so where two one-way centerlines share one roadway, as on the Riverway at Longwood or Edwin H. Land Boulevard, the stripes cross the whole asphalt rather than the owning record alone; a reservation or median wider than half a metre keeps them apart, and a cross street's junction mouth, a much wider street running alongside, or a record touching only another widened one (Bothwell Road's start beside Faneuil and Arlington Streets measured 49.8 m with alignment and overlap alone) leaves the stripes on the owning roadway. The station sits 8 m along the centerline itself, so a moved line's close taper vertices neither skip the crosswalk nor bend it. Because a record lives only in the tile that owns its road's midpoint, each tile also carries `borderPavements`: the records of other tiles' roads that run alongside a road the tile owns at one of its crosswalk stations (walked 8 m along the line from each end, as the renderer does, on the roads it stripes: 18 m or longer, 7 m wide, not alleys), candidates for that test (8 m in from a road end) by the renderer's own test, an edge crossing the station's across line within 40 m at a direction within cosine 0.8 of the road's, encoded like its own records (844 copies; Edwin H. Land Boulevard's two carriageways split between tiles 0_-2 and -1_-2), read for the crosswalk spans only while the owning tile draws the asphalt.

## Chelsea and the Tobin Bridge

Chelsea uses the same official surrounding-town boundary layer as Cambridge, Somerville, and Brookline. Its streets, building footprints, parks, and water context come from attributed OpenStreetMap extracts clipped to that boundary. The existing USGS 3DEP rectangle already contains the entire city; the rebuild grades Chelsea's new streets against that same 6 m terrain grid. Building heights follow tagged height/storeys or the documented type defaults. Chelsea Square, Bellingham Square, and Mary O’Malley Waterfront Park have safe road arrivals in the atlas.

Chelsea Overpass extracts use `out body geom`, because `out tags geom` omits relation members and silently loses multipolygon buildings and parks. The importer rejects incomplete Chelsea relation geometry. Hydration restored 316 building polygons, including six courtyard footprints, without duplicating any existing building ways; legacy-town extract semantics and their validated baseline stay unchanged.
